# The valley inside the globe — specification, first draft (20 Nov 2026; Stage 2 step 1 of `docs/MASTER-PLAN.md`); to be reviewed before any code

**Why.** The owner chose this (20 Nov) as the route to a population that lasts: the globe's squares cannot hold several separate bands (entries 114–116: camps at most about 8–12 squares apart; the bands shared one fate). A valley at walking scale lets bands live far apart in real terms, with their own food, water and hunters. The globe stays ("it gives the sense of my reality"). Evidence: `docs/reviews/2026-11-20-valley-basin-sources.md`.

**The stand-in.** The Awash axial valley at Gona/Hadar/Ledi-Geraru, Afar, Ethiopia, about 2.6–2.5 Ma: a reconstruction of one real kind of place, not a surveyed map. (Runner-up: Omo-Turkana Shungura C–E, for fauna.)

**What the evidence fixes, and how well.**
- **Vegetation:** a mosaic: about 50% grass on the floodplain (Gona isotopes: Quade et al. 2004; Levin et al. 2004); woody cover registered as a range of 30–50% (Cerling et al. 2011: most eastern African hominin sites under 40%); the densest trees and *Ficus*/*Tamarix* riverine woodland in a strip along the Awash; reeds and sedges at wet margins; open acacia/*Commiphora* steppe away from the river (Hadar pollen, secondary).
- **Water:** one permanent river (the Awash) as the anchor; seasonal pools elsewhere; free water at only one or two places in a large range in the dry season (Fongoli: Wessling et al. 2018): water scarce in the dry season; lake-margin salinity not verified: left out.
- **Stone:** cobble conglomerate beds near the river, sites on or beside them (Stout et al. 2005); trachyte, rhyolite, basalt and fine volcanics; hominins preferred better rock over the nearest; no distance figure found: a registered assumption (hundreds of metres to a few kilometres, from the general statement of "short transport"), tested at both ends.
- **Trees to sleep in:** in the river woodland; apes nest in gallery forest in the dry season (Ndiaye et al. 2013); no density figure: an assumption.
- **Animals:** grass-eating bovids dominate and open-habitat types rise from 2.7 Ma (DiMaggio et al. 2015; Plummer et al. 2015); the predator guild from `2026-11-18-predator-guild-sources.md` (hyena, leopard, *Homotherium*, *Dinofelis*, *Pachycrocuta*; no lions).
- **Not verified (to be stated on the page):** rainfall, temperature and the length of the dry season at 2.5 Ma; stone distances; australopith home range and group size; distances between neighbouring communities.

**The scale problem the evidence raises (the owner's decision).** At savanna chimpanzee densities (0.03–0.05 per km²: Ntakata, Ugalla) the planned 25 × 30 km (750 km²) holds only about 20–40 individuals: **about one band of 25**, not several. Savanna chimpanzee ranges are 63–85 km² or more (Fongoli): the valley is only 9–12 ranges. So either the valley is larger, or it holds one or two bands and several bands need a larger area. Options: (a) **about 50 × 60 km (3,000 km²) at 250 m squares: 200 × 240 = 48,000 squares, room for about 4 bands** (the cost: a world about 25 times larger in squares than the globe patch it replaces: profiling first; Stage 1 of the plan); (b) the planned 25 × 30 km at 100–250 m (12,000–75,000 squares) with one to two bands, and several bands as linked valleys; (c) a corridor along the river (long and narrow: 80 × 15 km) with bands strung along the water, which is also how savanna chimpanzees and baboons live; I recommend (c) or (a), to be decided after profiling the speed.

**Rules to rewrite (Stage 2 item 3).** Everything tied to "a square" or "a step" goes into metres and minutes, each with a switch back to the coarse world (the plan's rule): speeds, sight and smell ranges, home range, hunter range, food regrowth per square, rest and sleep, the 15-minute step.

**Test against real data (Stage 2).** Woody cover and habitat mix against Cerling et al. 2011 and the Gona record; distances to stone against Stout et al. 2005; the guild against Werdelin & Lewis 2013; the faithful-day table re-scored (it must not get worse); entry 84's escapes visible. **Stop rule (from the plan):** if the table falls by more than two rows and the cause is not found in a month, keep the coarse world for generation questions and use the valley only for learning questions.

**Questions for the reviewer.** (1) Is the Awash at Gona/Hadar the right stand-in, and is borrowing one place honest? (2) The scale: which of (a), (b), (c), given the cost? (3) What must be rewritten in metres and minutes first, and what is the smallest first build that already lets two bands live far apart? (4) Does anything here break the owner's rules (nothing gifted; no mechanism of our own without a source)? (5) Where does the valley's food, water and stone make the answer for us (building the answer in)?

## DECIDED (20 Nov 2026; the owner: "talk to opus and decide, im not sure"; Opus's review, the agent's report in the session)
**Scale: about 50 × 60 km (3,000 km²) at 250 m squares: 200 × 240 squares, built as a separate valley world that takes its weather from the globe.** Why: four bands of 25 at savanna chimpanzee density (0.03–0.05 per km²) need about 2,000–3,300 km²; ranges of 63–200 km² put band centres 15–25 km apart; the 750 km² of the first draft holds about one band. Not a corridor (twice the edge per km², so the made-up "elsewhere" rule would drive the result, and walls would push bands onto the river: clinging to the river must be the creatures' choice); not 100 m squares (300,000 squares; sight of 4–7 squares would be under 1 km); not two square sizes in one grid (a seam in every rule). At 250 m the sight gene's 4–7 squares become 1–1.75 km (plausible, cut by tree cover).
**Cost (all estimates; nothing run):** the squares are cheap (about 7 ms a step for 48,000 squares); the brains are the cost: hunters at real density over 3,000 km² (leopards 5.5 per 100 km², hyenas 3.5–11) are 270–660 against today's caps of 10, 8 and 6, and `preymap.js` shows one brained herd per square near every hominin and every hunter (thousands in the valley). 500–1,000 brains about 200–400 ms a step; a 33-year run 30–65 minutes; 24 worlds 3–7 hours on 4 workers.
**Smallest first build `--valley` (off = identical worlds):** a separate engine instance of 200 × 240, no east–west wrap, one latitude and one longitude (Gona about 11°N), weather from the globe square holding Gona plus local terrain, a river line with gallery woodland and vegetation from the specification (woody cover tested at 30% and 50%), all hominins in the valley, leavers to a ledger, one band first. **Rewrite first (they silently change meaning by about 144 times at 250 m):** attack reach (3 × 3 squares); food (fractions of a square → kg per km², eating in kg per step: new numbers that set hunger: source or register, both ends); prey map (`SQKM = 9`; one herd per square); movement (speed in metres per minute, several squares per step); "being together" (mating within one square, nursing, near ≤ 2, watching); hunters by area at field density, not per-world caps (`rescue` newcomers become arrivals from the edge); illness scaled with contact. Keep the 15-minute step. Can wait: fire, swimming, items and stones, sound carry, lightning, the retina experiment. Landscape: generated per seed from stated statistics (about 50% grass, woody cover a range, a river woodland strip, seasonal pools, cobble beds near the river); today's relief is a later, eroded landscape: at most the broad slope; add one fixed-landscape arm.
**Measure first (Stage 1 of the master plan, F8):** (1) profile one globe world today, split into squares, brains, senses and prey map; (2) time an engine built with W and H swapped (`engine-api.js build` swaps text in the source), no creatures; (3) count the herds shown per watcher. **Then, in order (predictions first):** switch off identical; one band of 25 in the valley (births per adult female, age at first birth, deaths by cause, the faithful-day table; predation judged against the primate median of about 3% a year; the band's yearly range against 63–200 km²); two bands about 20 km apart (some worlds should lose only one band); four bands; dispersal of young females.
**Not to do:** a corridor, 100 m squares, two square sizes in one grid; changing the step length now; today's relief as a 2.5 Ma map; adjusting food to reproduce the globe's rates; keeping per-world hunter caps; running four bands before one band passes its checks.
**The owner's five decisions, taken for them as asked (change any of them by saying so):** (1) 3,000 km² at 250 m: yes; (2) whether hunters far from any hominin may lose their brains: decided after the cost is measured (not before; the area is never shrunk for cost); (3) success is judged per band and per world; (4) one landscape per seed, plus one fixed-landscape arm; (5) arrivals from elsewhere: off by default, switched on only with a sourced rate, both ends reported.
**The valley alone is not expected to reverse the decline** (births 4.4% and deaths 6.0% a year): separate bands spread the risk; it must be shown, not assumed. **Signs the valley is making the answer:** survival changing across the registered range of food, woody cover, dry-season pools, stone distance or hunter density (leopard 0.25–12.7 per 100 km²); variation between landscapes larger than within one; a uniform valley with the same mean food giving a different result; the population lasting only when arrivals from elsewhere are on.

## V2 design (20 Nov 2026; Opus's audit of the valley branch, with `docs/reviews/2026-11-20-valley-scale-numbers.md`)
**Verdict: measure first, then make one change at a time.** In square units the valley already runs the globe's rules exactly, so "rules counted in squares" cannot on its own explain the 12 km spread. Likeliest causes, in order: (1) woody cover summed per square cuts sight to 2–4 squares (`senses.js` about 62: sight stops once summed cover passes 1.4; the gallery strip has 0.85 per square, the floodplain 0.3–0.5), and every adult pull toward others works only on what it can see; (2) nothing pushes back on exploring: the novelty reward (`sim.js` about 225, one per new 4 × 4-square block) pays in every direction because all the valley land is livable (the globe has sea, desert, cold); (3) water is only the one-square river and puddles, so thirst sends members on trips along it; (4) lightning is rolled per square (`weather.js` about 220): 144 times the globe's rate per km², bringing thunder startles and fires (unchecked). **Also:** 12 km is about the width of a savanna chimpanzee range (63–85 km²); a community that wide is chimpanzee-like fission–fusion: wrong only if mothers and infants split up or the spread keeps growing past one range.
**Rules secretly in kilometres already** (walking cost uses a fixed 3,000 m, `sim.js` about 1782; sound: distance × 3 km, `talk.js` about 63 and 113; lightning radius; prey map `SQKM = 9`): one metres-per-square constant `SQM` (3000 on the globe, 250 in the valley).
**Ordered steps (each behind a switch; off = identical worlds):** **V2-0 measure only** (log daily, in metres, for valley and globe: nearest-neighbour distance, party sizes, mother–juvenile distance, day path, 30-day range, steps with nobody near, river trips, lightning strikes and fires per km²); **V2a the SQM constant** (walking cost, sound, lightning radius, prey-map km²; no new numbers); **V2b speed and novelty together** (speed alone would multiply novelty rewards per step 13–27 ×; the novelty block size is invented: register it, test at both ends, do not fit it; a rule for the squares crossed on the way is an invented mechanism: register it); **V2c sight occlusion in metres plus the "near" sense** (honest occlusion may make dispersal worse: cohesion must then come from hearing, not a new pull); **V2d contact rules and attack to the same square** (births and kills may fall: do not compensate by changing the lust or attack odds); **V2e mother and child pulls in metres** (check mother–infant distance against field data); **V2f food in kg per ha** (the biggest lever on hunger and the biggest risk of building the answer in: source it or test at both ends; never fit it to the globe's rates); **V2g hunters by area, the prey map, newcomers from the edge, lightning per km²** (the brain cost). After every step: range per year against 63–85 km² (Fongoli; up to 200 per the spec) and the faithful-day table must not get worse.
**Can be left alone:** everything per step (the step stays 15 minutes: drains, decays, fear, sleep, digestion, rot, memory in days); the inborn starting weights in `brain.js` (directions only; inputs already scaled to sight range); climate (uniform forcing from Gona); fire spread (frozen), stone counts (Stage 4), swimming, the retina experiment. **Never:** add a "stay within X m" pull: that builds the answer in.
