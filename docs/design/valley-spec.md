# The valley inside the globe — specification, first draft (20 Nov 2026; Stage 2 step 1 of `docs/MASTER-PLAN.md`); to be reviewed before any code

**Why.** The owner chose this (20 Nov) as the route to a population that lasts: the globe's squares cannot hold several separate bands (entries 114–116: camps at most about 8–12 squares apart; the bands shared one fate). A valley at walking scale lets bands live far apart in real terms, with their own food, water and hunters. The globe stays ("it gives the sense of my reality"). Evidence: `docs/reviews/2026-11-20-valley-basin-sources.md`.

**The stand-in.** The Awash axial valley at Gona/Hadar/Ledi-Geraru, Afar, Ethiopia, about 2.6–2.5 Ma: a reconstruction of one real kind of place, not a surveyed map. (Runner-up: Omo-Turkana Shungura C–E, for fauna.)

**What the evidence fixes, and how well.**
- **Vegetation:** a mosaic: about 50% grass on the floodplain (Gona isotopes: Quade et al. 2004; Levin et al. 2004); woody cover registered as a range of 30–50% (Cerling et al. 2011: most eastern African hominin sites under 40%); the densest trees and *Ficus*/*Tamarix* riverine woodland in a strip along the Awash; reeds and sedges at wet margins; open acacia/*Commiphora* steppe away from the river (Hadar pollen, secondary).
- **Water:** one permanent river (the Awash) as the anchor; seasonal pools elsewhere; free water at only one or two places in a large range in the dry season (Fongoli: Wessling et al. 2018): water scarce in the dry season; lake-margin salinity not verified: left out.
- **Stone:** cobble conglomerate beds near the river, sites on or beside them (Stout et al. 2005); trachyte, rhyolite, basalt and fine volcanics; hominins preferred better rock over the nearest; no distance figure found: a registered assumption (hundreds of metres to a few kilometres, from the general statement of "short transport"), tested at both ends.
- **Trees to sleep in:** in the river woodland; apes nest in gallery forest in the dry season (Ndiaye et al. 2013); no density figure: an assumption.
- **Animals:** grass-eating bovids dominate and open-habitat types rise from 2.7 Ma (DiMaggio et al. 2015; Plummer et al. 2015); the predator guild from `2026-11-18-predator-guild-sources.md` (hyena, leopard, *Homotherium*, *Dinofelis*, *Pachycrocuta*; no lions).
- **Not verified (to be stated on the page):** rainfall, temperature and the length of the dry season at 2.5 Ma; stone distances; australopith home range and group size; distances between neighbouring communities.

**The scale problem the evidence raises (the owner's decision).** At savanna chimpanzee densities (0.03–0.05 per km²: Ntakata, Ugalla) the planned 25 × 30 km (750 km²) holds only about 20–40 individuals: **about one band of 25**, not several. Savanna chimpanzee ranges are 63–85 km² or more (Fongoli): the valley is only 9–12 ranges. So either the valley is larger, or it holds one or two bands and several bands need a larger area. Options: (a) **about 50 × 60 km (3,000 km²) at 250 m squares: 200 × 240 = 48,000 squares, room for about 4 bands** (the cost: a world about 25 times larger in squares than the globe patch it replaces: profiling first; Stage 1 of the plan); (b) the planned 25 × 30 km at 100–250 m (12,000–75,000 squares) with one to two bands, and several bands as linked valleys; (c) a corridor along the river (long and narrow: 80 × 15 km) with bands strung along the water, which is also how savanna chimpanzees and baboons live; I recommend (c) or (a), to be decided after profiling the speed.

**Rules to rewrite (Stage 2 item 3).** Everything tied to "a square" or "a step" goes into metres and minutes, each with a switch back to the coarse world (the plan's rule): speeds, sight and smell ranges, home range, hunter range, food regrowth per square, rest and sleep, the 15-minute step.

**Test against real data (Stage 2).** Woody cover and habitat mix against Cerling et al. 2011 and the Gona record; distances to stone against Stout et al. 2005; the guild against Werdelin & Lewis 2013; the faithful-day table re-scored (it must not get worse); entry 84's escapes visible. **Stop rule (from the plan):** if the table falls by more than two rows and the cause is not found in a month, keep the coarse world for generation questions and use the valley only for learning questions.
