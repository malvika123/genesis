# A realistic diet: leaves, pith, termites — Opus's design from the code, 23 Nov 2026 (build in the worktree `body`/`diet`); evidence `docs/reviews/2026-11-23-ape-diet-leaves-insects-sources.md`

**Verdict (Opus):** it can be built honestly in five small switched steps. Leaves are almost never eaten because the hominins **cannot see them** and fruit always comes first in the code, not because leaves are worth too little. Fix that before adding any new food.
**Why leaves are almost never eaten (code, worktree `body`).** Fruit always wins: when a hominin eats on a square the code checks fruit first, then roots, then leaves (`src/sim.js` 1031–1073); any square with trees thick enough for leaves (cover above 0.3, `leafy()`, sim.js 1865) also grows fruit up to 0.7 of its cover (fruit is zero only below 0.25 cover: `src/forest.js` 68–74), so leaves are reached only where fruit has run out. **Leaves are invisible:** the food sense in each direction counts fruit, cooked food and roots but not leaves (`src/senses.js` 270–277; the "food under my feet" sense 364–367), so the brain cannot learn to walk to leaves or tell a leafy square from a bare one. The "food at hand, stop searching" check leaves them out (sim.js 434); the strong hunger push counts leaves (366); the weak "try plants" pull (348) is the only pull toward eating leaves; fruit also has an inborn taste pull (8 × hunger, 341) that leaves lack. **The value is not the cause:** a leaf bite is 0.64 of a fruit bite (`config.js` 397: 7.8 against 12.2 kcal a minute). **A hidden gift:** leaves are never used up (sim.js 1066–1072): harmless today only because almost nobody eats them.
**Honest change.** Leaves become a real crop that can be eaten down (a seasonal flush of young leaves that is used up and grows back), shown on the existing food sense; then leaf eating in lean months comes out of learning with no script (when fruit is gone, leaves are the only food in sight and eating them relieves hunger: the brain's existing reward, sim.js 284–290). **No inborn pull toward leaves** (no evidence of an inborn taste). A protein appetite has some support (Uwimbabazi et al. 2021: chimpanzees keep protein steady while energy varies) but protein does nothing to the body yet: that waits for the body model.
**Insects.** Termite mounds stay put for years: each square gets a mound count from an area density (a registered assumption, per km² so it carries over to the valley); each mound has a small supply a hand can reach, used up by eating, back within hours; a jump at the start of the rains when winged termites swarm (from memory: verify); on the food sense like roots (`0.6*ROOT`, senses.js 272); eaten with the existing eat act after fruit; yield by hand near the leaf rate (feeding note H5, an assumption); protein roughly 35–60% of dry weight (memory: register it). **Left out:** termite fishing with a stick (the owner's call), ants as their own class, honey.
**Steps (each behind a switch; prediction first; off = identical worlds).** (a) `--diet-log` (measuring only): for each hungry hominin step: was there fruit, a leafy square, did it eat; the share of feeding steps by food class (prediction: over 90% of hungry steps in trees have fruit there; on leafy squares without fruit it eats under a third of the time). (b) `--young-leaves`: a young-leaf crop per square (flush timing from the fruit calendar: an assumption), used up and regrown, on the food sense and in the "food at hand" check (prediction: leaves 5–15% of feeding time, more in lean months; adult protein about 1.0 → 1.1–1.3 g/kg; little change in births). (c) `--pith`: pith and shoots at wet edges (sedge and reed squares, `src/items.js` 45, 100) and in gallery woodland; poor food (6.0 kcal a minute, about 0.49 of fruit), there all year, eaten when nothing better is around (3–12% of feeding time, late dry season). (d) `--termites` as above (1–5% of feeding time, peaking at the swarms). (e) roots and sedge corms already built (`SOC.sedges`, `rootVal` 80): check their share against 3–15%. (f) later: a protein appetite (a body drive that rises with the protein shortfall, not a pull to any food) once protein changes muscle or growth.
**Checks after each step:** feeding time by food class against living chimpanzees (fruit 50–80%, leaves 5–25%, insects 1–5%: use feeding time, not only protein share); protein per kg per day (adults at least 0.8; weaned children toward 1.2–1.6, do not push); daily feeding time 30–50% of the active day; hunger deaths and births; the faithful-day table must not get worse. **The gift test** (a new food too rich is a hidden gift): every new value at both ends of its range (leaves 0.48 and 0.64; termite yield and mound density low and high); if survival changes a lot across a range the evidence allows, the food is setting the answer: report it, do not pick a value; feeding time under 30% of the day or the new food far above the chimpanzee shares means too rich; a control arm with the same yearly calories but no seasons.
**Risks.** More food is more survival by construction (lean-month leaves should cut hunger deaths somewhat: real; a large change at the low end of the ranges is a warning); protein that does nothing (do not report protein gains as better fitness); the squeezed year (a "lean month" is 24 steps: flush and regrowth on the life clock); the 15-minute step (a step's yield must match the sourced kcal per minute); globe against valley (mound density per km²); **a new brain input would change every world even with the switch off: put leaves and termites on the existing food sense.** **Would not:** set target percentages or tune toward them; add an inborn pull to leaves or termites; keep leaves that can never be used up once they are on the senses; build termite fishing, honey or mature leaves; let the body choose fruit over leaves for the brain.
**The page, in the same step:** a hominin's panel shows the share of food eaten by class; log lines for the first leaf, pith and termite meals; mounds and the young-leaf flush on the map.
**Owner's decisions (my defaults, changeable):** (1) whether protein should have body effects (the strength build) before the protein appetite (f): yes, strength first; (2) termite fishing as a later tool step: later; (3) globe first or valley first: globe first (the valley's food is rewritten in kg per hectare later); (4) whether one shared food sense (leaves mixed in with fruit) is acceptable for now: yes for now (telling them apart later needs a new input, which changes every world).
**Could not verify (Opus):** what makes weaned children's protein so low (0.6 g/kg); whether hungry hominins stand on fruitless leafy squares (that is step a); leaf-flush timing in Afar woodland; termite mound density, yield by hand, energy and protein per gram; protein in pith; an inborn protein appetite in primates (Felton et al. 2009 from memory); whether the young-leaf and trichromacy idea (Dominy & Lucas) applies.

## The owner's point (23 Nov 2026): curiosity about new foods, not only hunger ("without a pull to eat it, aren't they curious about touching and eating? that's how we all try new things; a pull toward cow's milk and curd was not in our genes but we still tried")
**What the code does now (checked):** the only pull to put a plant in the mouth is hunger-driven and weak (`src/sim.js` about 348: `hu > 0.35`, `2.5 × (hu − 0.35)`, on roots and leafy squares); the curiosity rule (`a.g.c × S.cur`, `a.bored`, `a.novel`) rewards visiting new PLACES and speeds learning after something new, not tasting a new FOOD. **The owner is right:** real primates sample unfamiliar foods from curiosity when not hungry, the young most (mouthing and exploring), and copy what their mother eats; nobody is born wanting cheese: curiosity, learning from the result and copying came first (lactase persistence evolved later).
**Added to the design (step b0, before young leaves is useful):** a general **taste curiosity**: a small pull to put an unfamiliar food in the mouth, scaled by the individual's curiosity gene, stronger in the young, weaker when sated and when afraid; it is NOT specific to leaves or termites (no inborn pull to any one food: still the rule); the result (full belly, protein, sickness) teaches through the brain's existing reward; copying from elders and the mother carries the habit. Needs an evidence note first (primate food neophilia and neophobia, juvenile exploratory mouthing, how it declines with age and with hunger: not yet gathered) and a registered strength tested at both ends (0 = today). Requires the foods to be visible on the food sense (the fix above), or curiosity has nothing to act on.
