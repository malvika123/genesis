# Strength for the hold: the smallest honest build — Opus's design, 23 Nov 2026 (not built; waits for the hold test, entry 122)

**Verdict (Opus):** it can be built small and honest, but at one place only: the break-free chance in the hold. It will mainly change *who* breaks free (children and people attacked by big hunters less often, adult males a little more often) and barely change *how many* survive; the "ape strength" factor mostly cannot be told apart from the invented 0.3.
**Bodies today:** `bodyKg()` = adult weight (women 30 kg, men 42 kg; `SOC.massF`, `SOC.massM`) × a growth curve from wild chimpanzees (a child is about 6% of adult weight at birth, 30% at 5, 65% at 10, full at 13 for women, 15 for men; no fall in old age); `bodySize()` = weight ÷ 52 (adult woman 0.58, man 0.81; hunters: leopard 1.0 at 45 kg, hyena 1.1 at 60 kg, sabre-tooth 1.8 at 110 kg); 100 hit points each; speed from an inherited gene `s`, a growth factor and an old-age factor (halved over the last 30% of life). The attack roll uses the size difference, but every hunter outweighs every hominin, so the victim's body never changes the result.
**Where strength could go:** the break-free chance in the hold (direction supported: hunters fail more often against bigger prey; the value 0.3 is ours); **leave alone:** climbing (experts disagree: Stern & Susman against Ward; no strength number), carrying an infant (already handled by weight), fleeing speed (the speed gene), hitting power (no evidence of weapons at 2.5 Ma; the rally step is not built).
**Design (`--strength`, off by default):** strength = weight^(2/3) × the old-age factor the speed code uses (the 2/3 is the usual muscle scaling, Hill 1950 and Lietzke 1956, from memory: registered, tested at 2/3 and 1); no extra sex factor (Miller 1993's 52% is unchecked); break-free chance p = s / (s + c) with s = the victim's strength ÷ the hunter's weight^(2/3) (the standard two-sided contest form), c set so that a fixed **reference pair breaks free 0.3 of the time: an adult woman (30 kg) against a leopard (45 kg), written down before any run and never picked to match a result**. Gives: adult man against a leopard about 0.35; an 8-year-old (about 16 kg) 0.22; a woman against a hyena 0.26, against a sabre-tooth 0.19; the average goes down a little (hyenas and sabre-tooths outweigh the reference leopard). **Ape factor F (1.0–1.5):** O'Neill et al. 2017 gives 1.35–1.5 times human strength for chimpanzees; unverified for australopiths; measured against a hominin reference it multiplies every hominin equally and cancels: it only matters if the 0.3 is tied to "a 30 kg body with human muscle": then F = 1.5 lifts the reference to about 0.39, inside the 0.1–0.5 already tested. **Inherited or not:** step 1 follows the body (no new gene); a heritable strength gene (mutating like `s`) is a later switch; strength must never grow with use (CLAUDE.md). **Checks:** switch off identical to the current `escape` runs; switch on with every victim forced to the reference body identical to off (proves the anchor); hold off: strength does nothing; `npm test`; 24 worlds × 100 days, same seeds as entries 110 and 121.
**Predictions to write first:** children break free less often than adult women; adult men a few points more than women; free of sabre-tooths less often than of leopards; the young share of dead victims rises slightly; overall predator deaths: no change or a slight rise (not a fall). **Learning or dice?** strength changes only the dice; the victim must still choose to struggle: learning if the share of held victims who try to get away rises with age and after a previous attack; mechanical if it stays flat; also run brains that cannot learn. **Making the answer for us** if the main conclusion flips between the two ends of the exponent (2/3 against 1) or of F.
**Would not:** make hominins stronger than hunters or let strength hurt a hunter; touch the attack roll's size check, hunter strength or `atkP`; tie strength to climbing; add a strength gene in the same step; choose the reference pair or F after seeing results.
**Owner's decisions:** (1) whether F means anything at all given it cannot be told from the invented 0.3 (if yes: default chimpanzee-like 1.35 or human-like 1.0; test both); (2) whether old-age weakening reuses the speed rule (no source found); (3) the focus rule freezes everything except stabilising the population: this touches predation (the owner asked for it, 23 Nov: "yes do it"). **Could not verify (Opus):** Lietzke's and Hill's exponents; Miller 1993; any measured break-free or escape rate by victim size in primates; how strong carnivores are per kilo; how strength changes with age in chimpanzees; entry 122 itself.

## The owner's point (23 Nov 2026): strength is not the same for everyone; use and food matter; the ceiling can move over generations
"Not everyone is going to be strong, depends on their workout on a daily basis ... we can buff up and have unimaginable strength but the ceiling keeps slowing down due to generations of less activity ... their ceiling can be of real apes and much less, depends on the food, protein and muscle and fat and bones."
**Reading (Claude):** two layers. (1) **The ceiling**: an inherited trait (like the speed gene `s`): changes only by inheritance, mutation and selection, never by use (CLAUDE.md); a lifetime of training is not passed on; but the ceiling can still fall over generations by selection when strength stops paying and muscle costs energy (Bozek et al. 2014 on human muscle, unverified here; O'Neill et al. 2017 on chimpanzee muscle). (2) **Today's strength**: a body state that rises with daily effort (climbing, carrying, foraging, being active) and with good food (protein and energy: meat and other rich foods), falls with hunger, illness and age, up to the ceiling; the break-free chance uses this value. Both layers are real biology; only the first is inherited.
**What it needs that the sim lacks:** food tracks energy only (no protein); no muscle or fat state (the body layer: `docs/design/body-layer.md`); evidence for the rates (how fast muscle grows with effort, loses with hunger; what protein does; chimpanzee and forager muscle against activity: not yet gathered); each rate is an invented number to register and test at both ends.
**Staging (proposed, the owner to confirm):** stage 1 = Opus's simple design (strength follows body weight, one reference pair, tested at both ends of the exponent), to learn whether strength changes the outcome at all; stage 2 = the two-layer model (inherited ceiling plus a body state that follows effort and food), only if stage 1 shows it matters, after an evidence note on muscle plasticity and protein and a design review.
