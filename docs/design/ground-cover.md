# Ground cover you can see: shrubs, tall grass, boulders (plan item 33b) — 24 Nov 2026

**Owner's call (24 Nov):** shrubs, rocks and tall grass should be there because they make the world real, not because they change survival. Drawn only: no effect on the simulation (the `--more-cover` experiment of entry 134 stays off).

**What is drawn (flat map and 3D globe).**
- Shrubs: squares with thin to moderate woody cover (tree 0.04 to 0.55), 1 to 3 a square, most at middling cover.
- Grass tufts: squares where grass is thick (food over 0.45, not sand, not dense woodland); taller and more of them when the grass is thicker; they thin out when the grass is eaten or dries.
- Boulders: round rock squares (4 big ones) and stony ground (a couple of small ones).

**Evidence (live search 24 Nov; qualitative only).** Hadar pollen: montane evergreen bushland occupied the Hadar basin; wooded and grassy phases alternated (Bonnefille et al. 1987, 2004). Hadar paleosols: about 30% C4 grass and 70% C3 plants on the floodplain; Afar herbivore enamel: C4 grass 35% to 91% across the Plio-Pleistocene. Awash National Park today: Acacia-Commiphora shrubland with Acacia, Grewia, Psiadia and Vernonia shrubs; Aristida needle grass; overgrazed ground up to 50% bare soil and rock. Gona and Hadar: sediments interleaved with basalt flows 1 to 10 m thick; the rift floor is mostly basalt. NOT FOUND: shrub or boulder densities, grass heights, tall-grass species for the Pliocene. So how many of each are drawn is a drawing choice, not data.

**Test.** Chrome page check on both views (no errors, objects visible), not a sim test.

**Honesty changes (24 Nov, owner: "honest is what this project is").** Drawn only in the evidence region (the East African rift, about 25-50 °E, 10 °S-16 °N; everywhere on a made-up planet, which has no real geography); grass tufts on 30% of grassy squares (the Hadar paleosol share of C4 grass); a note on the About page says what is evidence and what is a drawing choice. Still drawing choices, not data: counts per square, sizes, one look for every bush.
