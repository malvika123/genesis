# Several bands (`--bands N`): the smallest honest build — Opus's review, 19 Nov 2026; not built; step 3 of the population plan (`docs/BACKLOG.md`, FOCUS)

**Verdict (Opus): build it, but small. Expect several bands to spread the risk, not to make the population grow.** Births 4.4% a year and deaths 6.0% (entry 113): the mean is about −1.6% a year; nothing about how people are grouped changes it. Bands that each shrink still shrink overall, they only take longer to die out: write that prediction before any run.

**1. Founding.** `src/world.js` already places extra founders after the base world (`--extra-people`, `USE2`, `makeBand(added)`); do the same N−1 times: each new camp from `homeSite`'s suitable squares, at least D squares from every other camp and at least 10 from every hunter; spawn after the hunters so band 1, the animals and the land are identical with the switch off. `BANDAT` holds one camp today: make it a list; hunter placement and `rescue` newcomers avoid all camps. Tag each person's birth band for measurement only (behaviour must never read it). Scale: on the real-Earth map a square is about 300 × 220 km (`src/earth.js`) but `src/preymap.js` treats it as about 3 km: D cannot come from real kilometres: measure one band's yearly range from existing traces, register D as about 2 × that range, test both ends. Cost about 4 ×: time 2 worlds first.

**2. Dispersal of young females.** Evidence checked: chimpanzee females leave at about 11 (Mahale), 11.4 (Taï), 12.6 (Kanyawara); not all leave (Gombe: about half of Kasekela's females and 1 in 5 of Mitumba's stayed: Pusey & Schroepfer-Walker 2013, PMC3826206); dispersers give birth about 2 years later (Walker et al. 2018); hominin: Copeland et al. 2011, *Nature* (strontium in teeth: small, probably female, australopiths more often non-local; sex inferred from tooth size); mechanism (Pusey 1980, Gombe): females avoid males they grew up with; transfer "apparently resulted from attraction to unfamiliar males". The code has no kin check and no inbreeding cost; loneliness gives no inborn pull toward people: so neither kin avoidance nor loneliness can make dispersal emerge. **Smallest honest mechanism:** a small memory of individuals she saw often before maturity; when cycling, her existing pull toward a ready male (mate-scent channel 6, `src/senses.js` about 312; the lust pull, `src/sim.js` about 351) is multiplied by f for familiar males (f registered, tested at 1 and 0, middle values allowed); an inborn pull the brain can override. **Scripting (not allowed):** "leave at age 12", aiming her at a band, moving her there, any rule that reads the band tag. Two existing numbers decide the result: the mate scent reaches 6 × sight range up to 60 squares (unchecked against any source: do not widen it); a female is "ready" only from age 14 (`smat`, registered from first-birth sources) while real females leave at 11–13.

**3. Merging of small bands.** Nothing makes a small remnant seek strangers (loneliness builds only when nobody is in sight; strangers pull only children, `src/sim.js` about 498, and sleeping company, about 408). **Do not build a "merge below 6" rule.** First count how often remnants of 5 or fewer join another band; if never, that is a finding; any fix is a later separate switch backed by evidence (vervet mergers came from groups already in contact).

**4. Predictions (write first).** 1 × 25: about −1 to −1.6% a year, about 5 of 24 emptied, predation about 2.7%, no gene flow. 1 × 100: same yearly change, about 0 of 24 emptied in 33 years; births per adult female rise (mates easier to find: only 25.5% of a ready female's time has a ready male near); per-person predation falls because hunters are fixed per world: dilution, not a real effect. 4 × 25 without dispersal: same per-person rates; fewer worlds emptied than 1 × 25; most bands still lost. 4 × 25 with dispersal: 50–80% of females breed outside their birth band at the near distance and about 0% beyond the scent range (a built-in control); males rarely move (flag it if they move as much); births later for dispersers; survival not helped by gene flow (inbreeding costs nothing here). 4 × 25 with hunters and illness per band: shows whether plain 4 × 25 is an artefact. **Artefact risks:** hunter caps (10/8/6) and `rescue` (below 4 per kind) are per world: more hominins means fewer hunters per person; far bands may never meet hunters; illness starts at a fixed rate per world (`disease()`), so 4 bands get a quarter of the outbreaks per person; if plain 4 × 25 predation falls below about 1.5% a year, or the gain disappears when hunters and illness scale with N, the result is not real; report emptied bands, not only emptied worlds.

**5. Not to do:** add inbreeding harm to make dispersal pay; tune f toward 50%; widen the scent range; add a merge rule; count 1 × 100 as a several-bands result.

**The owner decides:** (1) what a square stands for (sets D); (2) whether the success target in FOCUS is judged per band or over 160 years (else 1 × 100 or 4 × 25 meet "at most 1 world emptied" just by having more people in 33 years); (3) whether hunters scale with the bands' area (realistic, harsher); (4) whether to revisit the age-14 "ready" threshold.
**Could not verify:** the real range of the mate scent; Pusey 1980's full text; the Gombe 2-year delay at source; vervet merger details beyond the earlier reviews. Nothing was run: all timings are estimates.
