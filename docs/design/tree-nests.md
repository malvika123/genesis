# Sleeping up trees in the valley (plan item 32) — 24 Nov 2026

**Owner (24 Nov):** "i want them to live longer ... for a tradition down the line." Deaths at realistic speed are mostly predators (entry 138 follow-up: 85 of 113 in 6 worlds), 40% of victims asleep on the ground.

**Found.** Climbing needs `tree[square] >= 0.4`, a threshold set for the globe's 3 km squares (a forest); only 3% of 250 m valley squares reach it, and the dusk pull to climb is weak (2k, as large as the pull to sleep on the ground). So 6-7% of sleepers were up a tree. Real savanna chimpanzees nest in trees on 88-97% of nights (ground nests 3-12%, second-hand). Hominins: unknown; Lucy's arms and fall injury suggest some tree use (Kappelman 2016, Ruff; Johanson doubts).

**Switches.** `--tree-min X` (cover needed to climb; default 0.4) and `--nest-pull Y` (times the dusk pull; default 1). Both off by default: old worlds identical. Registered in docs/ASSUMPTIONS.md. Entry 139 tests 0.4/1, 0.05/1, 0.05/3, 0.05/6.

**Result (entry 139).** Up to 63% of sleepers in trees, predator deaths down about a fifth, population still falls 8% a year; trees protect only partly because leopards (and sabre-tooth, Dinofelis, set to climb) follow. Not reached: the chimpanzee share. Not run: pulls of 12 and 20; checking which hunters really climb.

**Next candidates.** Staying with the band (56% of victims were alone); mobbing a predator; checking `S.climb` for the extinct cats; a fall risk and nest-building cost (falls: none recorded).
