# Attack escape: why 70% of attacks are fatal, and the smallest honest build — Opus's design from the code, 20 Nov 2026; evidence `docs/reviews/2026-11-20-surviving-attacks-sources.md`; step 0 in build (branch `escape`)

**Why the owner asked (20 Nov):** apes, gorillas and monkeys survived 2.5 Ma of predators; "fleeing, hiding, confronting in groups; a fight to survive they must have learnt". Evidence: wild primates survive about 80–90% of leopard attacks (Taï: Boesch 1991, estimate 82–91%; the bloody-attack count 4 of 9 killed = 44%; Busse's baboons about 64% survived); predators kill a median of about 3% a year (Cheney & Wrangham 1987); rescue and mobbing seen (Taï: 11 rescues, 9 chase-aways in 5 years); weapons not supported at 2.5 Ma.

**Why about 70% of attacks are fatal here (Opus, `src/sim.js` 1417–1558).** One dice roll decides each attack: `pc = atkP 0.6` for an awake adult or `atkEasy 0.9` for a sleeper or an unweaned child, times the size odds (at least 1: hunters are size 1–1.8, hominins at most about 0.8) and ×0.35 up a tree; on success `t.dead = true` in that same step with **no wounding phase**; the 70% is those two numbers mixed by who is attacked (entry 110: 26% asleep, about 12% children: 0.6 × 0.62 + 0.9 × 0.38 ≈ 0.71: Opus's arithmetic, not measured). `atkP` and `atkEasy` are registered as unsourced "per 15-minute attempt" numbers (`docs/ASSUMPTIONS.md` 105) that F1 (alarm calls and vigilance) was meant to replace. A miss costs the victim 25 of 100 hit points, pain, rage and a little learning; the hunter cannot attack again for 4 steps; healing 0.05 hit points a step, so death from wounds needs about four misses. **Nothing the hominin does can change the outcome** except being up a tree (×0.35; a hunter that cannot climb, i.e. hyenas, cannot reach it at all) or awake, before the roll. **Group help does nothing:** a hominin cannot target a hunter (`senses.js` `edible()`: prey animals and fish only); the `help` term counts only the hunter's own packmates; a hunter is never hurt by a hominin and never retreats. **Suspected blind spot (to verify):** a hunter in the victim's own square is on no sense channel (the list of seen places skips distance 0), so the inborn flee and climb pulls (a hunter seen on channel 5) do not fire against a hunter right on top of the victim.

**Smallest build (each behind its own switch; off = identical worlds).**
- **(0) Measure only:** extend HUNTLOG for attacks on hominins (age class, asleep or up a tree, adults within reach, the victim's action, hit points, hunter in the same square); split the 70%.
- **(a) `--hold`:** sleepers and unweaned children as now (the evidence says they rarely get a chance); for an awake older hominin, contact becomes a bite plus a hold, no kill that step; next step the victim's brain chooses (climb, move, struggle), the hunter's brain chooses (press or leave: its hunger pull and full-stomach brake); a press kills only if the victim is still in reach and has not broken free. Reuses hit points, pain, fear, existing pulls and size odds. The chance of breaking free is invented: register it, test at both ends. A 15-minute step is far longer than a real attack (seconds to minutes): say so.
- **(b) climbing as escape:** the victim's climb is settled before the hunter's press; the tree rule stays (hyenas cannot follow; leopards kill at ×0.35: Busse 1980, a registered assumption); fix the blind spot (a hunter in the same square must be sensed: honest, the victim feels the grip).
- **(c) `--rally`, leopards only** (no primate evidence for hyenas or sabre-tooths: Fable): awake adults within reach, kin first (Boesch: mothers and males), get an inborn pull toward the victim and toward attacking; hominins become able to threaten a hunter that holds a victim; each threat costs the hunter harm and pain; the hunter's pull to leave when hurt decides whether it goes. Invented, to register and test at both ends: the pull's strength, the harm per threat, the hunter's pull to leave when hurt (needs a source that carnivores break off when injured). **Do not size it to Boesch's 45% bloody-attack ratio: that would be circular;** judge it by who dies (the young should die before help arrives).
- **(d) alarm calls and vigilance (F1):** an inborn call whose use is learned; listeners look and come closer; vigilance costs feeding time; F1 replaces the unsourced 0.6 and 0.9 with sourced detection and success rates. **(e) sleeping place, last:** leaving a sleeping place after a kill there (Anderson 1998); first find out why hominins sleep up a tree only 4.4% of steps (are big trees, tree ≥ 0.4, near camps?).
**Tests.** Measure: killed outright, killed after a hold, wounded and survived, escaped by climbing, by breaking free or by rally; by hunter kind, asleep or awake, child or adult; deaths per 1,000 hominin-years; worlds with each switch off identical. **Predictions first:** for awake adults deaths per attack fall from 0.6 (sleepers and infants stay near 0.9); hyena kills fall more than leopard kills; the share of victims who are young rises; deaths per 1,000 hominin-years fall from 27. **The attack rate:** do **not** raise attacks to hold deaths at 3% a year (tuning toward an outcome the other way): if deaths fall, report it and look for what is really missing (lions are not supported; *Homotherium*, *Dinofelis*, *Pachycrocuta*; hunters at field density by area; F1's real meaning of "an attack": today's 0.039 attacks per hominin-year against Taï's 0.30 is a different definition: the sim counts contacts that come to a roll, not charges; compare only after F1). **Learned or wired?** an arm with brains that cannot learn (if escape stays the same, the pulls do it); a hominin that survived one attack should escape its next more often; the pulls tested at 0 and at full strength; choices should vary and change with experience; helpers who have seen a rescue work should join more often.
**Would not:** lower atkP or atkEasy as a shortcut; a rule that hunters avoid groups (Boesch: group defence did not stop leopards); weapons or thrown stones; a rally against hyenas or sabre-tooths; an outcome lottery built from chimpanzee percentages; a full combat engine with sub-steps.
**The owner's decisions (taken, 20 Nov, and changeable):** replace `atkP` and `atkEasy` now (they are where the 70% comes from); the missing carnivores later (after bands); build on the globe branch `escape` now, the valley takes it later (the hold logic does not depend on square size; the valley's same-square attack rule V2d changes the reach from about 9 km to 750 m: building twice is the risk, accepted); whether hominins may hurt a hunter: only through the rally step (an inborn pull the brain can learn around), no clubs or sticks (no evidence at 2.5 Ma).
**Not verified (Opus):** Boesch's counts (the evidence agent read the paper: confirmed 29 / 9 / 11 / 9 / 44%); a source that carnivores break off when injured; whether sabre-tooths climbed; the same-square blind spot (read from the code, not run); the 70% breakdown (arithmetic).
