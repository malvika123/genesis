# What really protects wild primates from leopards, lions and hyenas: live-checked (24 Nov 2026)
Research agent; every row opened (PDF text or page). PDFs saved in the session scratchpad (papers/antipredator).
**Taï chimpanzees and leopards (Boesch 1991, Behaviour 117; eva.mpg.de PDF).** 22 confirmed encounters in 5 years, 4 deaths, 6 adults wounded; attack risk 0.30 per chimp per year, death rate 0.055 per chimp per year; "predation by leopard seems to be the first cause of mortality in Tai chimpanzees (39% of the 43 deads)". **Mobbing:** chased leopards away 9 times and rushed to rescue alarm-calling chimps 11 times; in one case 7 chimps chased a leopard until it was trapped and struck it with clubs (4.44 strikes per instance on average). **Party size:** "Party size decreased with an increase in predation pressure" (chi2 12.53, p<0.05); more all-male parties and fewer male-female ones; only 40% of Taï parties under 7; forest visibility about 20 m, and parties over 7 scatter beyond it.
**Leopards avoid noisy chimps (Zuberbuhler & Jenny 2002).** A radio-tracked leopard moved away or changed direction in 40% of 15 cases when chimps drummed or screamed nearby and "never approached a chimpanzee party"; only 1 of 200 leopard droppings held chimp remains; leopard kills track prey density, not group size.
**Sleeping.** Niokolo Koba, Senegal (Sylla 2022): nests mean 8.7 m high, range 1-30 m, 62% at 5-10 m, with lion, leopard and hyena present. Issa, Tanzania (Hernandez-Aguilar 2013): chimps prefer tall trees with high first branches; nest height steadier than tree height across dry predator sites (11.3-13.55 m); 2,167 nests. Fongoli chimps (fewer predators) nested lower and farther apart than Assirik chimps and sometimes on the ground (exact means not opened). Olive baboons, Mpala (Bidner 2018): slept at cliff sites on 79% of 1,253 group-nights; 87% of 100 leopard visits were to river sites where baboons were present only 8% of the time; yet "baboons are most often killed by leopards at or near their sleeping sites"; baboons also chase, injure and sometimes kill leopards by day. Guinea baboons at Simenti slept only in trees and predator encounters did not change where.
**Hominins (hypothesis only).** Swartkrans: 3 burnt bones among 153,781 fossils (Brain); his reading that fire at a cave entrance "would have given" protection from leopards is a hypothesis; SK 54 has leopard-sized punctures. Fongoli cave use is reported as being for heat, not predators (search result only).
**NOT FOUND ONLINE:** exact nest heights for Assirik and Fongoli; Fongoli night cave sleeping; Koops 2012 in full; Crockford 2012 numbers (it is about snakes); a Taï alarm-call rate to leopards and responders per event; vervet alarm response rates; Cowlishaw 1997 text; percent-of-time vigilance; leopard detection distance; Hamilton 1982 and Altmann sleeping numbers; Hart & Sussman.
**Protections the sim could add, best supported first:** (1) tall sleeping trees with high first branches, or cliffs (several sites; not perfect); (2) larger mixed-sex parties as sentinel strength, smaller parties when risk is high (Boesch: one population, tested); (3) group counter-attack and noisy mobbing (Boesch 9 chases, 11 rescues; leopards avoid noisy chimp parties in 40% of 15 cases; baboons injure leopards); (4) the cost: 0.30 attacks and 0.055 deaths per individual per year (Taï, rough); (5) fire or cave shelter (hypothesis, unproven for 2.5 Ma hominins); (6) NOT supported: alarm-call rates, vigilance percent, detection distance, cave sleeping for safety.
