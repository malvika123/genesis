# Is the 3.5-year limit on cracking nuts physics? (Fable, 13 Nov 2026; the owner's question)

Answer: **no — for nuts like those cracked by chimpanzees, strength does not explain it; coordination does.** A sourced physics
rule would stop only infants under about 1–2 years.

- Force to break the shell (slow squeeze): Coula 2.72 kN, Parinari 7.98 kN, Panda 9.6–12.2 kN (Peters 1987, in Visalberghi et al.
  2008, *Am J Primatol* 70:884–891); capuchins' palm nuts 5.2–11.5 kN (same). Oil palm: no number found.
- Strike energy: wild capuchins (1.9–4.4 kg) lift 0.9–1.9 kg stones 0.33–0.57 m and hit with 8.7–16.1 J, 1.8–3.9 × m·g·h
  (Liu et al. 2016, *Am J Phys Anthropol* 161:53–61). Weak strikers need more strikes, not fail outright (6.6 to 75+ strikes per
  nut: Fragaszy et al. 2010, *Anim Behav* 79:321–332).
- Why infants fail: at Bossou infants have the separate actions by 2.5 years but cannot order them; hammer-and-anvil use from
  about 3.5 (Inoue-Nakamura & Matsuzawa 1997); Taï: try at 2, hammer at 4, first nut by 5 (Boesch 2022). Capuchins first succeed
  at 25–29 months weighing about 1.8 kg, on nuts harder than Coula (Resende et al. 2008; Fragaszy et al. 2010). Body mass predicts
  efficiency (r = 0.75), but among animals of similar mass skill decides. No measurement shows strength stops a 3-year-old chimpanzee.
- Chimpanzee mass (Gombe, Pusey et al. 2005): about 10 kg at 5, 21–24 kg at 10, adults 31–39 kg. A 3.5-year-old (about 7.5 kg) is
  twice a capuchin that cracks harder nuts.
- Per-strike success is far lower than 0.83 (which is per nut): Taï adults about 7 hits per Coula nut (≈ 0.14 per hit); capuchins
  0.013–0.15.

Proposal (if hard nuts or stone size ever matter): grow body mass with age from the Gombe curve; strike energy E = k·m_stone·g·h
with sourced capuchin anchors; success scaled by min(1, E/E_need). E_need for Coula/oil palm has no source. For today's nut it
changes almost nothing after infancy.

Verdict: do not present the 3.5-year limit as strength. Relabel it as the observed age of onset whose cause (coordination) is not
modelled, or test dropping it (does a low beginner's chance plus practice put first success near 3.5–5 years by itself?). Still
unsourced: the energy needed, stone mass, the beginner's chance, the practice rate. The running test stays valid as a comparison,
but the limit may inflate the benefit of copying (strikes before 3.5 are never rewarded), so rerun, at least one pair, without it.
