# Plants and small animals of Hadar-Gona-Ledi-Geraru, about 3.4-2.4 Ma: live-checked (24 Nov 2026)
Research agent with live searches. "Opened" = page read; "snippet" = search result only. Only confirmed items may enter the world; everything else is a marked guess or left out.
**Plants (opened).** Hadar pollen 3.4-2.9 Ma (Bonnefille 2004, PMC514445): dominated by reeds (Typha), sedges (Cyperaceae; 2-60%) and grasses; dry-country trees and shrubs Commiphora, Acacia, Grewia and Capparidaceae; oldest sample with deciduous (Garcinia, Euclea) and evergreen forest or bushland (Alangium, Ekebergia, Juniperus); some highland trees (Myrica, Ilex, Hagenia, Olea). Setting: Acacia/Commiphora steppe with Ficus/Tamarix riverine forest in the Awash meanders. Rainfall then 800-1,200 mm a year (about twice today's about 500 mm); about 6.4 C cooler. Gona/Hadar (Quade 2004, experts.arizona.edu): early environments mostly forested with some grass, later floodplain more grassland; semi-arid and strongly seasonal for 3.4 Ma; about 34% grass before 2.9 Ma and about 50% at 2.7-1.6 Ma [snippet]. Ledi-Geraru (news.asu.edu, 2017): wet and wooded about 3 Ma to dry and open at 2.8 Ma; almost all animals fed on grass. Mille-Logya younger units: open savanna or grassland with little woody cover (PMC7237685).
**Small animals (opened).** Mille-Logya: catfish (bagrid and clariid), a large ostrich, a duck or goose, crocodile (incl. Euthecodon), turtles, snails and mussels, ostracods, rodents (no detail). Hadar (Wikipedia, citing Campisano 2022; secondary): spur-winged goose, crowned crane, darter, ostrich, crocodile, python, monitor lizard, viper, mud terrapin. Dikika: giant otter Enhydriodon dikikae, otter Lutra hearsti, Nyctereutes lockwoodi. Insects: Laetoli (about 3.5 Ma, a NEIGHBOUR site) trace fossils of a moth, dung beetles, nine solitary-bee taxa and termites [snippet].
**Plant looks (opened, Wikipedia).** Balanites: greenish-yellow flowers, green fruit ripening brown, edible but bitter, ants take nectar. Vachellia tortilis: small white, highly aromatic flowers; flat coiled pods. Ficus sycomorus: figs ripen buff-green to yellow or red, grows along rivers, fruits all year, heaviest July-December.
**Analogue diets today (snippets only):** Fongoli chimps, 77 plant species (fruit, flowers, pith, cambium, seeds); Ugalla chimps about 100 plant species, more roots and tubers than forest chimps; savanna baboons: Acacia, grasses, tubers, grass corms.
**NOT FOUND ONLINE:** fossil wood, fruit or leaf finds; phytoliths; leaf-wax numbers for Afar; Omo-Turkana pollen genera; Lates and tilapia in the Afar record; frogs; flamingo, storks, guineafowl; named freshwater mollusc genera; bats; rodent species; fossil bees, honey or butterflies in Afar; a woodland cover percent for Hadar; month-by-month chimp fruit seasons; baboon diet at Awash; colours for Commiphora, Combretaceae, Salvadora, Ziziphus, Capparis, Grewia, Boscia, Tamarix, Typha, papyrus, palms; phenology of Acacia and Ficus on the Awash.
**What may go in the world (confirmed only):** reeds and sedges at lake edges and shallows; fig and Tamarix on the banks; Acacia (white coiled pods), Commiphora, Grewia, Capparis-family shrubs on dry ground; Balanites; patches of Olea, Juniperus, Euclea bushland in wetter spells; grass about a third of cover before 2.9 Ma and about half by 2.7; strongly seasonal rain; ostrich, spur-winged goose, crowned crane, darter, crocodile, terrapin, python, monitor lizard, viper, catfish, snails and mussels; dung beetles, solitary bees and termites flagged as from a neighbour site.
