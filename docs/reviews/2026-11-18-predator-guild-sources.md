# Which large carnivores lived in East Africa at about 2.5 Ma? (18 Nov 2026) — research agent, read only; summaries not full texts

**Summary:** at about 2.5 Ma, *Homotherium*, *Dinofelis* and *Chasmaporthetes* are supported (moderate confidence); **lions are NOT supported** (no secure fossil lion before the Pleistocene); crocodiles were present but predation on hominins at 2.5 Ma is unsupported.

| Kind | Present at about 2.5 Ma in East Africa? | Confidence | Evidence of preying on primates or hominins |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lions (*Panthera leo*) | No (the Laetoli lion-like cat, about 3.6 Ma from memory, named *P. principialis* 2023, is a separate early *Panthera*; no Ledi-Geraru lion; "first African lion about 1.9–2 Ma" not checked) | moderate–high | none |
| *Homotherium* | Yes: *H. hadarensis* (Petter & Howell 1988) Hadar, about 3.45–2.95 Ma; *H. ethiopicum* Omo; early Koobi Fora (African species poorly defined: Turner 1990) | moderate | none found |
| *Dinofelis* | Yes: *D.* cf. *aronoki* at Hadar (Maka'amitalu, Hadar, Busidima); East African record from about 4 Ma, extinct in Africa about 1.4 Ma (Werdelin & Lewis); South Africa *D. barlowi* about 2.7–1.98 Ma (Adams et al. 2016) | moderate | Brain (1981) "specialist primate killer" at Swartkrans (South Africa); isotopes (Lee-Thorp, Thackeray & van der Merwe 2000, *J Hum Evol* 39:565) put the one tooth with C4-grass-eating prey: not proven either way |
| *Chasmaporthetes* (hunting hyena) | Probably: extinct before 1.5 Ma (Werdelin & Lewis 2013); no East African site or date found; South Africa at Sterkfontein about 2.46–2.01 Ma | low–moderate | none |
| Crocodiles | Present; *C. anthropophagus* at Olduvai Bed I about 1.8 Ma (Brochu et al. 2010, *PLoS ONE*): hominin bones with marks nearby, not shown to be killed; Braun et al. 2010 argue against heavy predation | moderate | none at 2.5 Ma |
| *Pachycrocuta* (giant hyena) | East Africa about 3.0–2.6 Ma (Alba et al. 2013, secondary) | moderate | — |
| *Megantereon* | mid-Pliocene (Treves & Palmqvist 2007); East African date not checked; likely hominin predator in South Africa (Lee-Thorp et al. 2000) | low–moderate | South Africa |
| Large eagles | Taung child (about 2.0 Ma, South Africa), crowned eagle (Berger & McGraw 2007): disputed | — | disputed |

**Guild richness (Werdelin & Lewis 2013, *PLoS ONE* 8:e57944, verified from full text):** large carnivorans of 21.5 kg or more per time slice: 3.5–3.0 Ma 19; 3.0–2.5 Ma 17; **2.5–2.0 Ma 15**; 2.0–1.5 Ma 15; **modern 6**. Functional richness fell nearly 99% since 3.5 Ma. The sim has 3 kinds. No predation rate on early hominins was found anywhere. Carnivore damage on hominins: SK 54 (Swartkrans, about 1.8–2.0 Ma) punctures fit leopard SK 349.

**For the sim:** the sim's "sabre-tooth" is best read as *Homotherium* (East Africa, supported); *Megantereon* an alternative (not verified). Candidates to add behind switches: *Dinofelis* and *Pachycrocuta* (moderate), *Chasmaporthetes* (low–moderate); **not lions**; crocodiles only as a water danger at most, not as a hominin predator, until evidence says otherwise.
**Could not verify:** first African lion date; any carnivore list for Ledi-Geraru or Gona at 2.8–2.2 Ma; *Chasmaporthetes* and *Megantereon* East African dates; *Crocuta* first appearance; OH 7; pythons; Brain 1981 itself; the Werdelin & Peigné 2010 reference (inaccessible); any predation rate.
