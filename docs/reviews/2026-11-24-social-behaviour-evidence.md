# Grooming, play, consolation, reconciliation in apes and baboons (24 Nov 2026)
Research agent with searches; only Romero 2010 opened in full, the rest are abstracts or recalled (marked). Order of confidence: grooming, reconciliation, consolation, play.
**Citation warnings.** "Wittig 2008" is about wild BABOONS (Horm Behav 54:170); the chimpanzee stress paper is Wittig 2016, Nat Commun 7:13361. "Brooker 2026" not found (unverified). Silk 2010 Curr Biol not found (unverified); Silk 2009 found strong female bonds linked to longer-lived offspring; a later study (Silk 2018) suggests the longevity link may be an artefact.
**Grooming.** Wild chimps about 5-15% of the day (Wrangham 1992 via secondary; Romero 2010 captive 5.5% females, 7.1% males); baboons and Old World monkeys about 10-20% (Dunbar 1991; Lehmann, Korstjens & Dunbar 2007, Anim Behav 74:1617), ceiling about 20% of daytime. Partners: kin, close affiliates, higher rank; reciprocity (high confidence). Stress: lower urinary cortisol with bond partners (Wittig 2016, high). Strong female bonds raise baboon infant survival independent of rank (Silk, Alberts & Altmann 2003, Science 302:1231, high for that population).
**Play.** Infants about 10-18%, juveniles about 10% (low-medium, one secondary note); peaks in infancy and juvenile years (Cordoni & Palagi 2011; Heintz 2017, Gombe). Costs (energy, injury, exposure) recalled. Benefits for motor and social skill are DEBATED: correlation only.
**Consolation.** Wild chimps: bystander contact to the victim of aggression: grooming 28%, embrace 27%, gentle touch 19%, kiss 10% (Romero, Castellanos & de Waal 2010, PNAS 107:12110; 3,003 conflicts, 10-minute windows); females, kin and strong affiliates more; less after reconciliation. NO overall rate per conflict found (the guess of 5-15% is unsupported). Empathy reading debated: model the contact, not the feeling.
**Reconciliation.** Exists in chimps, bonobos, baboons, macaques (high). Captive chimps often 20-50% of conflicts, wild lower (about 5-20%, recalled, low-medium). Valuable partners reconcile more (Aureli, Cords & van Schaik 2002; Preuschoft 2002, medium-high). Reduces renewed aggression and stress (recalled).
**Recommendation.** Grooming: budget about 5-15% of waking time (cap 20%), bouts of several 15-minute steps, partners weighted by kin, friends, rank; cost = less foraging and slightly lower vigilance; benefit = stress and fear down, bond up. Reconciliation: former opponents within about 10 minutes, rate 10-40% of conflicts, higher for valuable partners. Consolation: free parameter below reconciliation, kin and friends, test sensitivity. Play: young only, about 10-20% of waking time (low confidence); do NOT hard-code skill gains.
**Would refute.** Removing grooming changes nothing in stress or survival; reconciled pairs show no less repeat aggression; contacted victims recover no faster; play-deprived young show no deficit.
**Not verified.** Exact wild time budgets; gorilla and bonobo grooming; bout lengths; consolation per conflict; wild reconciliation percent; play percents; play costs.

## Live-checked addendum (24 Nov 2026; "opened" = page read; "snippet" = search result only). This section OVERRIDES the recalled figures above.
- **Reconciliation, WILD:** Taï chimps 188 cases of 876 conflicts = 21.5%; bystander affiliation first act in 164 cases (18.7%); authors say probably undercounted by about 50% (Wittig 2010 PLoS ONE, journal.pone.0013995, opened). Mahale chimps mean corrected conciliatory tendency 14.4% (Kutsukake & Castles 2004, abstract). Chacma baboons: aggressor grunts to victim after 9% of disputes (N=10), a close relative of the aggressor after about 20% (N=21), authors estimate 30-40% combined (Wittig 2007, PMC2124468, opened). Wild bonobos: lower than captive, no percent found (snippet).
- **Consolation:** Romero 2010 chimps are in OUTDOOR GROUPS, not wild (earlier note corrected): acts were grooming 28.1%, embrace 26.8%, gentle touch 19.2%, kiss 9.5%; no per-conflict rate. Wild Taï: bystanders affiliated with the victim in 26 of 109 cases (about 24%, infant and juvenile bystanders excluded; Wittig 2010, opened).
- **Play, wild Gombe infants:** social play peaks near age 2 at 15.16% of observation time; solitary play peaks 21.78% at age 1, below 5% by age 4 (Lonsdorf 2014 PLoS ONE, opened). Juvenile and adult play percent: NOT FOUND.
- **Grooming:** bout length wild Mahale males mean 153.6 s (median 95.5 s), bidirectional 197.5 s (Kaburu & Newton-Fisher 2016, opened); bonobo sessions mean 14 min (snippet). Time: review says wild chimps 5.7-11.7% of the day, up to 33% at feeding sites (snippet, secondary). Dunbar: about 20% of time seems an upper limit for social time (opened, preprint); human group size would need about 42%. Gombe infants groom 0% in the first six months rising to 3.07% at 4.5 years (opened).
- **Stress and bonds:** lower urinary glucocorticoids with bond partners in wild chimps (Wittig 2016, abstract, no effect size). Giving grooming (not receiving) linked to lower faecal glucocorticoids in Barbary macaques (2007, abstract).
- **Infant survival and bonds (CHALLENGED):** Silk 2003 links strong bonds to infant survival (abstract); a later Amboseli re-analysis (preprint 2024, abstract only) finds that after accounting for mothers' changing behaviour maternal sociality "is no longer positively associated with infant survival". Silk 2010 does exist (Curr Biol, PMID 20598541, female longevity); Silk 2009 (Proc R Soc B, PMID 19515668) is the offspring-survival paper. Cheney & Seyfarth 1989 is vervets, not baboons (snippet).
- **NOT FOUND ONLINE:** wild chimp grooming percent of day at named sites; wild gorilla grooming; wild bonobo percent of day; savanna baboon grooming percent; wild bonobo reconciliation percent; baboon consolation rate; cortisol effect sizes; juvenile and adult play percent; australopith or early Homo grooming-time predictions.
- **Consequence for the build:** use wild numbers: reconciliation about 14-22% of conflicts in chimps (30-40% in baboons by the authors' estimate), consolation about 24% (Taï), social play about 15% of time at age 2, grooming 5.7-11.7% of the day with a hard ceiling near 20% for all social time. Do NOT build "bonds raise infant survival" as a fixed rule; it is a contested hypothesis.

## Full-text addendum (24 Nov 2026): read from PDF text with pdftotext; page numbers approximate. Overrides snippets above where they differ.
- **Taï conflicts by cause (Wittig & Boesch 2003, Table 3):** female-female social 49%, food 51%; female-male social 40%, food 45%, sex 15%; male-male social 76%, food 16%, sex 8%. 1,071 conflicts with full information, 876 analysed; no per-hour rate (only 80 male and 123 female all-day follows). Feeding takes about 45% of the Taï time budget.
- **Contact vs non-contact:** large rank gap 81% non-contact, middle 63%, small 64%; no overall split; no injury counts in the paper (intensity scored by likelihood of injuring).
- **Post-conflict help (Wittig & Boesch 2010, PLoS ONE 5:e13995):** a bystander offered affiliation to one opponent in 164 of 876 (18.7%), authors say "very likely underestimated ... by 50%" because only one opponent was followed; of 109 adult cases 83 went to the aggressor, 26 to the victim. Strong evidence for relationship repair, moderate for consolation.
- **Injuries, Amboseli (Archie 2014, BES 68:1183):** 707 injuries to 160 of 231 adult females over 29 years = 0.46 injuries per female per year (about one every 2 years); no share of conflicts causing wounds. Gombe: 70% of 23 injuries with known cause attributed to adult males (cited).
- **Grooming (Lehmann, Korstjens & Dunbar 2007, Table 1; group size, % of day):** bonobo 27.8, 5.7%; eastern chimp 59.2, 11.67%; western chimp (Taï) 40.33, 8.27%; anubis baboon 58.8, 8.3%; chacma 28.07, 12.64%; hamadryas 51, 13.5%; western gorilla 11, 0.09% (personal communication); mountain gorilla 6, 1%. Grooming time rises with group size up to about 40, then levels off; up to about 20% of the day for the most social species.
- **Infant survival and sociality (Silk 2003, Science 302:1231):** 16 years, about 34,000 focal samples of 108 adult females over 633 female-years; sociality positively associated with infant survival independent of rank, group, environment (the later re-analysis was NOT found in full text).
- **Still NOT FOUND:** conflicts per hour; how conflicts end (shares); overall contact share; share causing wounds; male aggression per hour at Kanyawara, Gombe, Ngogo; play budgets (Heintz 2017, Lonsdorf 2014 not downloaded); forager within-camp quarrel frequency; the 2024/2025 Amboseli re-analysis. PMC pages blocked curl (bot check).
