# Feeding time and intake rates (18 Nov 2026): what the evidence gives

Research agent, read only, for the feeding time budget (backlog "FIRST TOMORROW"). **Enough numbers to set intake per step: partly** — plant foods, daily feeding minutes and daily energy need are sourced; per-minute rates for meat, nuts, termites, honey and
dug tubers could not be verified.

**Main finding.** Kanyawara (Kibale) wild female chimpanzees took in about **8.8 kcal per minute of feeding** and fed about **304–314 minutes a day** (about 42% of a 12-hour day; the 12 h is the agent's assumption). A 15-minute bout gives
about 130 kcal, about 7–9% of a day's need. In the sim (measured 18 Nov, seed 9, real Earth, 10 days): eating took 2% of adults' awake steps and the **median eating step added 12.8 energy units, about 3,100 kcal** — nearly two days' need.

Daily intake in the preprint: v1 2,433 ± 1,221, v2 3,029 ± 1,198 kcal/day (v2 adds fermented fibre); read from abstract text; no peer-reviewed version found: provisional.

**Daily energy:** doubly-labelled water, chimpanzees and bonobos pooled (Pontzer et al. 2016, *Nature* 533:390–392): females 1,722 ± 363 kcal/day at 46.4 kg, males 2,145 ± 546 at 57.9 kg (mostly zoo or sanctuary). Scaled by mass^0.75 to
30–40 kg: about 1,300–1,800 kcal/day (assumption). 1,500 kcal at 8.8 kcal/min needs about 170 minutes (24% of a 12-hour day); wild chimpanzees feed longer, partly because gross intake ignores poor digestion, travel between patches and lean
seasons: a net yield of about 5.5–7 kcal/min fits about 37% of the day.

**Daily feeding time:** Kanyawara females 304–314 min/day; Waibira 36–37% (from the earlier search; the paper itself blocked here: Villioth et al. 2025, *Am J Primatol* 87:e70104); Fongoli rests more and travels less in the hot dry
season (Pruetz & Bertolani 2009, abstract only). Baboons, Amboseli dry season: wild-feeding adult females fed about 60% of the time and rested about 10%; females at a garbage dump fed 20% and rested almost 50% (Altmann & Muruthi 1988,
*Am J Primatol* 15:213–221) — richer food sharply cuts feeding time.

**Recommended hypotheses:** H1 a 15-minute eating step of average plant food gives 5–7% of a day's need (refuted if adults' yearly daily feeding time falls outside 30–50% of the active day); H2 by food — ripe fruit 7–11%, young leaves 4–7%,
pith 3–6%, seeds 2–3% (refuted if fruit-rich seasons do not show shorter feeding time than leaf-heavy ones; fruit days gave about 1.4× the energy of other days); H3 open country: poorer savanna days push feeding time up toward the baboon
60% (refuted if savanna agents feed less than forest agents for equal food); H4 meat (assumption): at most about 10–25% of a day's need per step, raw meat about 1.3–1.5 kcal/g (not checked; refuted if yearly meat intake per adult exceeds
about 50 g/day); H5 nuts, tubers, termites, honey provisional: nuts at the fruit range, termites at the leaf range, no tuber digging above the fruit rate (assumption only).

**Could not verify:** Waibira 36–37% (blocked); feeding percentages at Fongoli, Issa, Ugalla, Assirik; Günther & Boesch 1993 kcal/min (book chapter); Hadza tuber kcal/h (Hawkes, O'Connell & Blurton Jones 1989; Kraft et al. 2021);
chimpanzee meat intake per minute and carcass eating time ("chewing an infant baboon for nine hours", Wrangham & Conklin-Brittain 2003, second-hand); termite and honey rates; active day length; differences by sex and age.
