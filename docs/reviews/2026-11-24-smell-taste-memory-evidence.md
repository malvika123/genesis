# Smell, taste and memory in primates: evidence for the build (24 Nov 2026)
Research agent with real searches (13 tool calls). Extends docs/design/smell-droppings.md (carcasses, wind, droppings are covered there).
**Verdict.** Smell and taste can be built honestly as a SHORT-RANGE sense tied to memory, but no detection distance, learning speed or memory duration for apes or hominins is sourced: those are flagged assumptions, tested at 0.5x and 2x.
**Findings (confidence).** Apes and Old World monkeys have about 32% broken smell genes vs about 17% in monkeys without full colour sight: a gene count, not an ability; direction solid, numbers disputed (Gilad 2004, PMC314465; Dong 2009 reanalysis) - medium. Primates still smell rot-type chemicals below 1 part per million (Laska 2007, J Exp Biol 210:4169) - medium, captive. Spider monkeys tell ripe from unripe fruit odour (Hernandez Salazar 2015, Sci Rep) - medium. Wild capuchins sniff more as fruit odour rises (Melin 2019, Nat Commun) - medium. Wild chimpanzees check figs by sight, then touch or smell, then teeth (Interface Focus 2016) - medium-low. Ripe fruit carries low ethanol 0.005-0.48% (Dominy 2004) - medium. Smell matters most for fruit, less for insects, little for leaves (Nevo & Heymann 2015) - medium (abstract only). Mandrills avoid faeces of infected animals by smell (Poirotte 2017, Sci Adv, PMC5384805) - high for mandrills. Chimpanzees without Toxoplasma avoid leopard urine; infected ones do not (Poirotte 2016, Curr Biol; 33 chimps) - medium, correlational. Chimpanzees prefer sweet, thresholds near human (Hladik & Simmen 1996) - medium. Tannin puts apes off sugar from about 3-6 mM (Simmen 2003) - low. Chimpanzee bitter receptors vary a lot between individuals (Hayakawa 2012; Wooding 2006) - medium. Bitter Vernonia eaten as possible self-medication (Huffman & Seifu 1989) - hypothesis. Smell memories more emotional in humans (Herz & Schooler 2002) - medium; the "Proust effect" is argued to be ordinary cue memory (Chu & Downes 2000). One-trial learning of cues in marmosets, motion-sickness aversion in squirrel monkeys - medium-low.
**Recommendation.** Smell short range, built only in the fine valley (100-250 m squares): strength by source type (ripe fruit, carcass/rot, predator urine or dung, own group, smoke), spreading downwind; detection range an ASSUMPTION (tens of metres for fruit, under 200 m for a strong carcass). Taste needs contact: four channels (sweet/savoury, bitter/astringent, sour/rotten), each a NOISY reading of real content, each animal with its own bitter sensitivity. Memory: store smell/taste with its outcome (reward, sickness, fear) AND the place; illness forms in one trial with hours of delay allowed; rewards need several trials; fear and sickness memories last longest (decay an assumption); mild first-sniff caution for new foods. Innate (supported): sweet liking, strong-bitter dislike, rot/faeces aversion. Learned: which smells mean which food, which bitter foods are safe, dangerous places, kin smells. Predator smell: weak evidence, so only mild innate wariness.
**Would refute.** Apes finding fruit or carcasses from hundreds of metres; wild sniffing rare or unrelated to ripeness; no infant sweet preference; naive apes ignoring predator scent; hominins repeating a poisoned food after one illness.
**Not verified.** Any detection distance in metres; Laska fruit-odour thresholds; Nevo et al. 2015 as named (not found); primate memory durations; wild one-trial numbers; kin/oestrus smell; carcass sniffing by hominins; smoke avoidance.

## Live-checked addendum (24 Nov 2026, pages opened; "snippet" = search result only)
- Fruit smelled from 4-15 m, only with the wind blowing towards the animal: ring-tailed lemurs (sciencefocus.com/news/scentsational-skill-lemurs-can-sniff-out-fruit-hidden-15-metres-away). The ONLY primate distance found; not apes.
- One-trial learning with colour and shape cues: squirrel monkeys and marmosets (Laska & Metzker 1998, PMC313805). Odour cue remembered up to 4 weeks, marmosets only; visual cues kept after 4 months (snippet).
- Turkey vulture: cues from "several hundred feet", distance "not determined with certainty" (avianreport.com/turkey-vulture-sense-of-smell).
- Fire: wild savanna chimpanzees (Fongoli) calm, avoided fire only as it approached, good at predicting its path; elephants highly stressed (sciencedaily.com/releases/2009/12/091222105312.htm). Learned prediction, NOT innate fear. More than 75% of their range burns yearly (snippet).
- Wild chimp sniffing: no distance given (PMC6234885). Chimps travel from farther to trees that bore the best fruit before (Ban 2014, eva.mpg.de press page); fruiting gaps 2 months to 3 years (Janmaat 2013, snippet).
- Rot-smell thresholds below 1 ppm, some animals below 1 ppt (Laska 2007, snippet; page blocked).
**NOT FOUND ONLINE:** any distance at which wild apes or monkeys smell fruit, carcasses, predators or each other; predator-scent tests on wild monkeys; baboons using wind; Laska fruit thresholds for chimps and humans; any primate taste or odour memory over 4 weeks; odour-place memory in wild primates; carcass-smelling distance in km for vultures, hyenas, lions or primates; any innate fear of fire or smoke.
**Consequence:** every smell distance and memory duration stays an assumption (tens of metres for fruit is the lemur 4-15 m figure stretched; test 0.5x and 2x).
