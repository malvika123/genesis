# Group size and predation on primates: live-checked (24 Nov 2026)
Research agent; every row below was opened (PDF text or Europe PMC XML). PDFs saved in the session scratchpad (papers/groupsize).
**Real community and troop sizes.** Chimpanzees (Bertolani 2013 via PMC6612355 Table 2): Gombe 51, Kanyawara 48, Budongo 46, Ngogo 143, Tai North 35, South 63, Middle 11, Fongoli 32, Bossou 20, Kahuzi 23; chimp communities average 35, known maximum 150 (Ancrenaz et al. 2015); Tai group 61.1 (Zuberbuhler & Jenny 2002 Table 1 footnote). Bonobos 10-120; gorillas median 10 (Ancrenaz). Chacma baboons Moremi about 70 per troop, 20-100+ in general (Busse 1980); olive baboons Laikipia 58-63 (Isbell 2018 Table 1). Hunter-gatherer bands: Ache about 28-31 and Eastern Hadza about 19 per band (own division of Hill et al. 2014, PMC4105570); mean 28.2 adults in 32 foraging societies (Hill 2011) came from a summary, NOT the paper. Dunbar's human prediction 147.8 (95% limits 100.2-231.1), exploratory.
**Does a bigger group cut predation?** Across species, higher-risk monkey populations have larger groups (Hill & Lee 1998, 121 populations; abstract only). WITHIN species the evidence is against it: Isbell 1994 "cannot reliably separate the effects of body size and group size" and within-species attempts "have failed"; Kenyan vervets and olive baboons: no link, the larger baboon group had a predation rate "not any lower" (Isbell 2018); Tai monkeys: predation higher on bigger groups (r2 = 0.39, P = 0.098, not significant); Boesch on Tai chimps: detection "through increased vigilance and number of eyes is minimally increased by group size", and chimps respond by foraging in smaller parties.
**Real predation rates (these matter for judging the sim).** Tai chimps and leopards (Boesch 1991): attack risk 0.30 per chimp per year; death rate 0.055 per chimp per year (5.5 per 100 chimp-years; 9-18% of attacks may kill); 17 of 43 deaths (39%) attributed to leopards (an estimate from the death rate, not counted bodies); chimpanzee in only 1 of 200 leopard droppings (Zuberbuhler & Jenny 2002, Table 2; primates 32% of droppings). Median yearly predation across 24 primate populations about 3% (range 0 to over 15%; Isbell 1994). Laikipia olive baboons: leopard predation 0.02 a year (4 of 227), 0.18 on nights leopards were near. Moremi chacma: lions and leopards the main cause of adult mortality (Busse 1980; 14 attacks, 5 kills seen).
**Compared with the sim (entries 124-126, per 1,000 hominin-years):** predator deaths 24.2 with 6 hunters and 21-35 in earlier runs, against real 30 (the median of 24 primate populations) and 55 (Tai, the high end). So the sim's base predator deaths are INSIDE the real range, not above it. With 12 hunters (entry 127/128, partial) about 53: Tai's level.
**NOT FOUND ONLINE:** Hill 2011 full text (camp vs band sizes); any hominin group-size estimate (Aiello & Dunbar 1993, Layton 2012, Grove 2012); Hill & Lee 1998 effect sizes; Cheney 2004 (the 97% figure is a snippet, unverified); Cowlishaw 1994 (403); predation share of adult deaths at Gombe and Amboseli; site sizes for Mahale, Ugalla, Issa, gelada, hamadryas, yellow baboons.
**Consequence.** (1) A bigger band is NOT supported as a way to cut predation: within a species group size does not reliably lower it. (2) A band of 25 is inside the hunter-gatherer range (19-31) and below the chimpanzee mean (35); a change to 35-50 is justified only as community size, not as a fix, and must be tested and reported as such. (3) The sim's predator deaths are not too high: the population problem must be in births, other deaths, or in what protects real primates that the sim lacks.
