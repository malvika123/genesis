# Numbers for the watching rule: peering rate, age, context, distance to the mother (17 Nov 2026)

Research agent, read only. **Enough numbers to set the watching rule: partly.** A peering rate, the ages of peak peering, whom and in what context; no published bout length and no exact distance-to-mother tables for chimpanzees.

| Measure | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Chimpanzee peering, definition | looking at another's task from under 2 m for more than 5 s | Nodé-Langlois et al. 2025, *Commun Biol* 8:509 (full text) |
| Peering rate, Taï | 2,343 events from 35 immatures under 10 y in 1,873 focal hours: about **1.25 an hour** (division by the agent; mixed ages; not stated in the paper) | Nodé-Langlois 2025 |
| Context | **92% while foraging**; about **3 times higher during extractive tool use** than ordinary feeding | Nodé-Langlois 2025 |
| Age | peaks at **4–5 y**; number of different elders watched peaks at 5–6 y (12 per 60 h), 7 per 60 h at 9 y | Nodé-Langlois 2025 |
| Whom | mother **over 80%** of targets in year 1, **under 20%** after 9 y | Nodé-Langlois 2025 |
| Closeness | elders the youngster spends about 10% of time within 1 m of are about **3 times** more likely to be watched than those at about 0% | Nodé-Langlois 2025 |
| Orangutans, wild | peaks at 3–4 y, falls with age; watching the mother falls with age; range 0–2 m | Kukofka et al. 2025, *iScience* 28:111542 (full text) |
| Orangutans, overall | 1,537 events in 2,571 h, about 0.6 an hour | Schuppli et al. 2016 (abstract snippet only: **not verified**) |
| Budongo chimpanzees | 366 events in >1,000 h, under 0.37 an hour | Slania et al. 2026 |
| Distance from mother, Ngogo | more than 5 m away in 50% of scans at 4.5 y, 60% at 5 y | Vlaeyen et al. 2026, *Dev Sci* 29:e70102 |
| Distance from mother, Gombe females | in contact 50% of the time at 1–2 y; more than 5 m away only 25% at 5–8 y; riding on 50% of travel until about 3 y | Lee et al. 2020, *Am J Phys Anthropol* (figures); via Vlaeyen 2026 Table 1 |
| After weaning | juveniles stay with mothers "almost constantly" for several years | Pusey 1983, *Anim Behav* 31:363–377 (abstract snippet) |
| Other adults | mean daily party size 4.2–7.5; Gombe females alone or only with dependants 40–70% of the time (cited background) | Murray et al. 2014, *PNAS* 111:18189–18194 |

**Could not verify:** Lonsdorf 2005/2006 watching shares (paywalled; only "daughters watched more"); Inoue-Nakamura & Matsuzawa 1997 rates (3 infants, 150 episodes); Biro 2003 beyond the 3–5 y window; Estienne 2019 watching numbers;
peering bout length (only the >5 s threshold); chimpanzee time within 1 m / 5 m of the mother by age (figures only, Lonsdorf 2014 Fig. 6).

**Inference (the agent's, not sourced):** at a rate λ an hour at random times, P(a 15-minute period holds a bout) = 1 − e^(−λ/4): λ 0.35 → 8%, 0.6 → 14%, 1.25 → 27%. Peering bunches during feeding and tool use, so: ages 3–6 about 10–25% of
periods together; ages 0–1.5 and over 8 about 3–10%; during extractive tool use up to about 40%.

**Recommendation (each a hypothesis):** (1) N = 2 steps (30 min) together in the square — weakly supported; refuted if non-mother watching is not much rarer than mother watching early on (target: mother >80% in year 1,
<20% by 9 y). (2) The elder must be foraging; tool use counts about 3×; refuted if removing the condition changes nothing or watching happens mostly outside feeding. (3) Both awake, the youngster not travelling;
refuted if data showed peering during travel (none do). (4) Watching a chance per step once 1–3 hold: about 0.15–0.25 at 3–6 y, 0.05–0.10 at 0–2 and 7–10 y (about 0.4–1.3 events an hour together); refuted if the
peak falls outside 3–6 y or the rate outside 0.3–1.5 an hour together. (5) Weight elders by closeness, the mother above all, about 3× between rarely-close and often-close; refuted if watching is spread evenly.
