# Colour vision, bright things and curiosity in primates (23 Nov 2026) — research agent, read only; several items from memory

**One line:** Old World monkeys and apes see red-green colour as we do, so the hominins can honestly be given it; an inborn liking for one colour is not supported, but a pull toward things that stand out and are new is plausible (partly unverified).

**Reading.** Australopiths descend from apes that already had red-green vision, so giving them our colour channels is faithful (inference; no fossil evidence). The case for fruit is weaker than for young leaves (the one direct field test found no trichromat advantage for fruit at close range; brightness mattered more). **Could not verify:** a strong inborn orienting to bright or moving things in primates (general knowledge only); warning-colour avoidance in primates (innate or learned); infant preference for red; the age decline in exploration; object play in young chimpanzees; Fedigan et al. 2014; Regan et al. 2001; Sumner & Mollon 2000; Jacobs's duplication date and the chimpanzee result (memory); any sourced detection distance for fruit or leaves; direct fossil evidence of australopith colour vision.
**Recommendation (the agent's).** (1) **Eye report with no labels:** for each visible object three numbers: brightness, a red-green value and a blue-yellow value, plus size and angle in view; a distance limit based on object size; no invented exact distance (a tunable setting marked unsourced). (2) **Leaves must be visible:** young leaves are red-shifted against mature leaves (a sourced reason): a red-green difference, not a "food" tag. (3) **Innate:** attention pulled toward large contrast, sudden change and movement; fear of snake-shaped things learned from watching others with a head start for snake shapes. (4) **Learned:** what a signal means ("red means sweet"): each hominin starts with no link from colour to food; it forms by tasting and watching others. (5) **Novelty pull** rising with young age (the decline with age is unverified: keep the curve a hypothesis), a personal openness trait, low fear and moderate hunger; fear (the neophobia side) pushes the other way; fades as the thing becomes familiar. (6) **Register as hypotheses:** H1 red-green contrast improves finding young leaves; H2 openness varies between individuals; H3 novelty seeking falls with age. **Unsupported, do not build:** an inborn liking for a specific colour or food (refuted if naive hominins chose red items over others). **Tests:** remove the red-green channel and see whether leaf finding drops; run naive animals with no learned colour link and check they do not prefer red.
