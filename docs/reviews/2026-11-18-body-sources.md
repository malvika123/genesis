# Early hominin bodies: bones, muscle, locomotion (18 Nov 2026) — research agent, read only; abstracts and snippets only

**One line:** only a few body numbers are solid enough to use now (size by sex, a cost-of-walking range, chimpanzee muscle about 1.35–1.5 times human); fracture rates and Lucy's cause of death are too contested to model.

**Recommendation (the agent's):** body mass by sex and age (afarensis female about 29 kg, male about 45 kg; range 20–30 kg for females; dimorphism an adjustable assumption); one climbing-against-walking trade-off (about 6 J/kg/m at about 1 m/s, speed cap about 1.3 m/s, cost above modern humans; a climbing factor labelled a hypothesis); relative muscle strength as one tunable setting (1.35–1.5 × modern); optional later: fracture risk from the about 20% healed-fracture figure (low confidence, healing time unsourced). **Do not model yet:** Lucy's death, throwing power (erectus only), swimming or buoyancy, bone density or growth, limb-length numbers, a muscle-mass percentage, arm-strength numbers.
**Could not verify:** Jungers, Ruff, Holliday limb numbers and the intermembral index; Lucy's 1.07 m; Lovell 1990 and Jurmain exact figures; fracture healing times; chimpanzee muscle fraction; Lucy's humerus robusticity; Pontzer 2012; curved phalanges; Gordon 2008's exact ratio; bone growth and density; swimming.
