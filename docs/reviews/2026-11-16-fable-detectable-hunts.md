# Fable reviews detectable hunts (staying-alive step 2), 16 Nov 2026

Read-only review; every source marked by how it was read (asked for after an earlier wrong citation). Summary kept in full in the
session's notes; the key points:

**Verdict.** Do not build step 2 as written yet. Faithful for stalking cats (leopard; sabre-tooth as a labelled stand-in), not for
hyenas (they run prey down and fail by being outrun; their 36% is near the field's one-third: leave them as now). The detection number
could not be sourced today, and entry 90 already shows two faults (leopard meat, sabre-tooth kills) that detection would hide or worsen.

**The code.** A hunter waits 4 steps after an attack (at most one an hour). The target is the first edible animal in the 3×3 squares,
no choice by size or watchfulness. Base success 0.6 (0.9 asleep or young) × size odds has **no registry row**. A failed attack bites
(up to 25 health); a death "of wounds" counts as a kill but the hunter swallows nothing and keeps no memory of the place.

**Smallest faithful rule (later, for leopard and sabre-tooth only).** Asleep: detection 0 as now. Awake: one detection chance from
field data on hunts lost to detection, at most two levels (head down after eating, head up); fear above 0.25 = the top level (a herd
stays alert after a failed hunt with no new timer). A detected attack makes no contact (no bite, no wound, no fight-back); the prey's
fear rises and its flight pull acts; the hunter still pays its hour. Vigilance share is not detection chance: any formula joining them
would be our invention. Until full texts are read, detection is a registered assumption tested at both ends.

**Field values** (read today: Stander & Albon 1993 full text — lions 15–38%, detection on open ground and moonlit nights; Hayward et
al. 2006 *J Zool* 270:298 full text — leopards 5%, 16%, 38%, stalk 4.4 m, rush 10.3 m; Smith & Holekamp chapter — hyenas about one
third, Kruuk 1972 calves 15/23/31% by group size, chases to 4 km). Leopard kills 0.14–0.18 a day, about 22 kg a kill, 4.0 kg a day;
lioness 8.7 kg a day dry season, 14 wet (Stander 1992, abstract); jaguar 0.23 and puma 0.12–0.18 kills a day as sabre-tooth stand-ins.

**Trace first.** Leopard (6.7 kg a kill against ~22): prey mix (hares 1 kg of flesh), kills by wounds (nothing swallowed), the rest
of a carcass (no hoisting; others take it), attacks on buffalo, cubs counted as hunters, days far from prey. Sabre-tooth (0.86 kills a
day): stomach and energy store at each attack (a 27.5 kg stomach emptied in 24 h passes about 3 × its need; the store caps at 100),
killing when not hungry (the fixed pull), prey mix and carcasses left, the 350 kg preferred size and 8.4 kg need (assumptions).

**Wrong reasons to guard against.** Success lowered by a chosen multiplier; most attacks on sleepers (then the fault is how antelope
sleep); hunters starving and refilled by the newcomer rule; detection moving attacks onto young, sleepers and hominins; detected
attacks still biting; hyenas "right" only because unchanged.

**Registry rows needed.** Base success 0.6/0.9 (missing); detection head down/up at both ends; which kinds stalk; a detected attack
makes no contact; alertness through fear > 0.25; hyenas unchanged and why. Size about one week.

**Left out (the owner's list).** Cover and grass height, moonlight, wind, day vs night; choosing the least watchful, edge or lone
animal; choosing prey by size; many eyes, mixed herds, alarm snorts, stotting; hyenas' long chases and weak-prey choice, young hyenas
poor until 5–6 years; hunts given up before the rush; leopards hoisting kills; theft at field rates; prey leaving the area after a
meeting; light, short sleep in antelope; lions, *Dinofelis*, *Homotherium*; detection for hominins as prey (a separate change).
