# Caves and early hominins (18 Nov 2026): what the evidence says

Research agent, read only, at the owner's rule: factual reality over anything. **Did early hominins use caves at 2.5 Ma: no evidence** (and the South African cave sites point to caves as predator places, not homes). Confirmed by search: Pruetz 2007,
Berna 2012, the Swartkrans carnivore debate, the Wessling 2018 heat note; the rest is from memory and marked.

**Early hominins.** The South African cave sites (Sterkfontein, Swartkrans, Kromdraai, Drimolen, Malapa; about 3–1 Ma): Brain (1981, *The Hunters or the Hunted?*) argued the hominin bones there were prey; leopards, at some sites hyenas and eagles; bones dropped or washed into
caves that already worked as predator lairs and death traps; Pickering et al. 2004 (several carnivores contributed); Martin et al. 2024 (*S Afr J Sci*: *Homo* was leopard prey at Swartkrans). None argues hominins lived there. Malapa (memory: Dirks et al. 2010, *Science* 328:205):
deep pits animals fell into. Swartkrans burnt bone (memory: Brain & Sillen 1988, *Nature* 336:464): fire at about 1–1.5 Ma, debated. East African sites (Olduvai, Koobi Fora, Hadar, Bouri, Gona) are open-air beside lakes, rivers, floodplains; no cave evidence known (memory, not searched).
**Earliest well-supported cave use:** Wonderwerk Cave, South Africa: burnt bone and ash in early Acheulean layers at about 1.0 Ma, probably *Homo erectus* (Berna et al. 2012, *PNAS* 109:E1215; one cave; use, not necessarily living there). Gesher Benot Ya'aqov is open-air (memory: Goren-Inbar et al. 2004, about 0.79 Ma).
Secure cave occupation with hearths much later, about 0.4 Ma and after (Qesem, Neanderthals). Zhoukoudian not checked (disputed). So about 1 Ma at the earliest, regular use later: **1.5 million years or more after the sim's 2.5 Ma.**

**Living primates.** Fongoli chimpanzees (Senegal): caves used mainly in the hottest part of the year, much cooler than open ground; evidence mostly indirect (feeding traces, dung, hair, records 2001–2006); other causes may add (Pruetz 2007, *Primates* 48:316–319); late dry-season temperatures above 45 °C
(Wessling et al. 2018, *Front Ecol Evol* 6:60). The only well-documented ape cave use found: hot-weather cooling, not sleeping or rain shelter; they still nest in trees (memory). Baboons sleep on cliffs; Chacma in caves (Brain, memory; rates and purpose not verified). Leopards and hyenas use caves
(Brain and others); no source for rates of predation on primates inside caves.

**What to model (agent's recommendation).** (b) **thermal only**, no sleeping pull and no shelter pull from rain or wind at 2.5 Ma: remove "strong cover like a big tree" and the pull for sleepers; keep the thermal effect modest (cooler in the hot dry season, used by choice when hot; an inference from
modern chimpanzees); a mild predator pull for leopards and hyenas is defensible but rests on no rates: keep weak and flagged, or leave out; no cave pull for the sabre-tooth (not verified). **Refuted by:** evidence of *Australopithecus* or early *Homo* sleeping or living in caves at 2.5 Ma (none found);
a study showing Fongoli chimpanzees do not cool off in caves; a study showing predators avoid caves or that East African caves are not cooler.

**Could not verify:** Brain 1981's text and Zhoukoudian; baboon cave rates; *Megantereon* cave denning; East African cave records (no systematic search); Wessling 2018 page numbers.
