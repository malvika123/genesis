# Muscle, protein, effort and the strength ceiling: real numbers for the body model (23 Nov 2026) — research agent, read only; only the items marked "checked" were confirmed by web search; "memory" items are leads to verify

**One line:** muscle can plausibly rise about 0.5–1% a week with hard effort and fall 0.3–0.6% a day with total disuse, but almost every primate number is thin, so most of the model must be registered as an assumption and tested at both ends.

**Recommendation (the agent's).** (1) Ceiling inherited, heritability about 0.5–0.6 (range 0.4–0.75); early hominins a mass-specific force ceiling about 1.0–1.5 × human (a reasoned guess, not a measurement). (2) Muscle fraction females about 35–40% of body mass, males about 40–50% (captive bonobos: wild may be a little higher); fat a working range 3–12%. (3) Rates for 15-minute steps (96 a day, 672 a week): 1% of muscle a week is about 0.0015% a step; 0.5% a day about 0.005% a step; gain 0.25–1% a week with high effort and enough protein, zero if protein or energy is short; loss 0.1–0.5% a day for severe disuse or starvation; about 0.5% a year from age 30, about 1% a year after 60; loss faster than gain. (4) Protein: 0.8 g/kg a day to maintain, 1.2–1.6 for growth, capped at about 1.6; chimpanzees and early hominins plausibly ate more per kg (leaves and bulk): unverified. (5) Starvation: fat first, then muscle (a quarter of the weight lost is lean when fat is plentiful, rising as fat runs out). (6) Register and test at both ends: all food protein values, the gain rate, the disuse rate, the climbing effort needed to hold muscle, the 1.0–1.5 × ceiling, the fat fractions; test zero and maximum effort, zero and twice the protein, heritability 0 and 1.
**Could not verify:** wild against captive chimpanzee strength or muscle (no study found); wild chimpanzee body fat and muscle; Hadza protein share of energy; any direct estimate of australopith strength or when the human muscle change happened (Bozek et al. 2014 shows human muscle chemistry changed along the lineage; timing unchecked); exact bed-rest, training and sarcopenia rates; chimpanzee vertical metres and time budgets.
