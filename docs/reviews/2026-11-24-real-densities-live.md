# Real densities of the Awash-type cast: live-checked (24 Nov 2026)
Research agent; each row opened (PDF text or page) unless marked (snippet). PDFs in the session scratchpad (papers/density).
**Large carnivores (per 100 km2).** Leopard: 6.81 +- 1.24 in Acacia-Commiphora near the Great Ruaha River (Ruaha NP, camera traps 2018-19); 4.23 +- 1.02 whole study area; 3.2-3.4 in miombo; Serengeti 5.41; Namibia 14.51 (fenced Okonjima) and 0.59-3.6 (open farmland and parks, PMC6826368). Spotted hyena: Ruaha 10.80 +- 1.08 (prey-rich); Serengeti 34-214.5; Amboseli 165; Ngorongoro 89-143; Mara 95; Kruger 13-53.6; Chobe 40; Hwange 38.9; Mole 12; **Senkele Swayne's Hartebeest Sanctuary, Ethiopia 95 (call-ups, 2018)** (Searle 2023 supplementary Table 6; mostly call-up methods). Lion in Awash NP today: 0.014 +- 0.001 per km2 = 1.4 per 100 km2, now only in the north (2024 survey). Not found: wild dog, cheetah, brown and striped hyena, caracal, serval, jackal densities.
**Awash National Park, transects (Enawgaw thesis, 2003/04; 756 km2 then).** Beisa oryx 10.38 per km2 (21.46 in 1992), Soemmerring's gazelle 1.58 (5), lesser kudu 1.09 (2.64), warthog 3.28 (6.43), Salt's dikdik 7.36 (14.05); all artiodactyls 23.69 animals and 2,271 kg per km2, 80% of the weight oryx; greater kudu and Swayne's hartebeest not seen in 2003 (84 greater kudu counted in 1992). 2024 survey: oryx 0.184 and gazelle 0.016 per km2 (park 591 km2; only a third suitable). The park has lost much of its game since 1969: today's numbers are a degraded modern park, not Pliocene.
**Water animals (per km of river).** Hippo: 26.9 overall, 3.4-48.1 by reach (Mara, 2006); about 26.6 Zambezi (snippet). Nile crocodile: 1.4-3.1 (lower Zambezi spotlight counts), 0.9-5.4 by area (opened); Okavango Panhandle 1.63 (snippet).
**What hunters eat (Hayward et al. prey-preference papers, 41 sites for leopard).** Leopard prefers 10-40 kg prey (best about 25 kg); baboon 2.8% of kills against 5.1% of available prey; spotted hyena prefers 56-182 kg (mode 102 kg), baboon 0.75% of kills against 26.1% available; wild dogs 16-32 kg and 120-140 kg, baboon among the avoided. So **hominin-sized prey (30-45 kg) sits in the leopard's preferred weight range but primates are taken less than their availability; hyenas and wild dogs hardly take them.**
**NOT FOUND ONLINE:** baboon troop density (only home ranges in snippets: Filoha band at least 38.6 km2; GPS study 64-86 km2); Carbone & Gittleman 2002 full text (the 90 kg per 10,000 kg rule is only a search summary); Hempson 2015, East 1984, Coe 1976, Fritz & Duncan 1994, Ogutu & Owen-Smith 2003; elephant, rhino, giraffe, buffalo, zebra, wildebeest, hartebeest, impala densities; per-km2 hippo; Awash hippo, crocodile and baboon counts; leopard density specifically in riverine habitat; Lake Turkana crocodiles.
**For the sim.** Real densities would put about 20 leopards (6.8 per 100 km2) and 15 to 40 spotted hyenas (3.6-10.8, but 95 in an Ethiopian sanctuary) in a 20 x 20 km (400 km2) zone, against 2 of each now; the sim's hominin targeting would have to be tuned against the measured primate predation rate (about 3% a year median; Tai 5.5%).
