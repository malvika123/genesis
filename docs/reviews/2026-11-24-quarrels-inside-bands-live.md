# Quarrels inside a band, apes and foragers: live-checked (24 Nov 2026)
Research agent with live searches; "opened" = page read. Many sources were blocked or were PDFs; two PDFs were decoded by hand.
**Conflict rate.** Taï chimpanzees (Wittig & Boesch 2003, eva.mpg.de PDF, opened): 1,071 complete dyadic conflicts from 203 all-day follows of single chimps (80 male, 123 female), about 5.3 conflicts per follow-day (our own division; hours per day not stated; not a per-hour or whole-community rate). Community 18 adults, 4 adult males. A conflict = a dyadic aggressive exchange ending in submission, flight, reciprocal screaming or non-aggressive behaviour. Ngogo females (PMC3890096): 0.26 aggressive acts per 100 h towards at least one other adult female (feeding 0.19, non-feeding 0.33); a rate per aggressor while another female is present.
**Causes and severity (Taï, opened).** Three contexts: food, sex with oestrous females, social/dominance; males fought more in social contexts and females more over food (shares not read). Large rank gap: 81% of conflicts used non-contact aggression; medium or small gap 63-64%. Dominant initiators won 92%, subordinate initiators 60%; draws under 4%. Making up between high-benefit partners: 63.6% of conflicts with a high conciliatory score vs 23.8% for medium-benefit partners.
**Injury.** Amboseli baboon females: 707 injuries in 160 of 231 adult females over 29 years (Archie 2014, decoded PDF); wound risk highest near ovulation. Gombe baboons: males wounded more than females, both most at rank-competing ages (abstract). Ngogo chimps: males the main participants in lethal attacks, two lethal attacks seen (PMC8277110).
**Foragers and early hominins (snippets only, not opened).** Two thirds of 148 lethal cases in 21 mobile forager societies began within the group (Fry & Soderberg); !Kung serious disputes about every 2-3 weeks at large waterholes (no named source); no secure case of within-group violence in australopiths (Brain's review).
**NOT FOUND ONLINE:** quarrels per hour at any site; shares by cause; wound and death rates from within-group aggression in wild chimps; baboon agonistic rates per hour; Kanyawara and Gombe/Mahale/Budongo rates; time and stress cost; how conflicts end (shares); food sharing and tolerance in apes; foragers' quarrels per day.
**Consequence for the build.** Enough to start a within-band quarrel rule with ONE sourced anchor (about 5 conflicts per animal per day at Taï, mostly mild, most without contact when ranks differ, most won by the dominant) and every other number an assumption to test. Better numbers need a PDF reader (poppler) or sources that do not block bots.
