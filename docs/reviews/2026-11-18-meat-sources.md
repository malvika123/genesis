# Meat: eating rate, chimpanzee intake, hominin evidence (18 Nov 2026) — research agent, read only

Method note: Nature, PubMed, PMC and PeerJ blocked fetches; everything below is from search snippets and press coverage, **unconfirmed against the primary texts**. Someone with institutional access should read the primaries.
**Best-supported raw-meat eating rate: about 400 kcal/hour (about 6.7 kcal/min)** — only in secondary summaries of Wrangham & Conklin-Brittain 2003 (*Comp Biochem Physiol A* 136:35–46; "optimistic", partly from one chimpanzee chewing an infant baboon for 9 hours); no range given; no measured chimpanzee or human kcal/min or g/min for raw meat found.

**Eating rate.** Scientific American: *Homo erectus* would need 5.7–6.2 h/day of chewing at that rate; the abstract says most raw meat is too tough to chew easily. Carmody & Wrangham 2009: wild chimpanzees chew 4–6 h/day, humans under 1 h/day (all food, not meat). Zink & Lieberman 2016 (*Nature* 531:500): with meat one-third of the diet, chews fall by almost 2 million (13%) and chewing force 15% (abstract as reported; press says 17% and 20%); raw goat "turns into a clump"; chews or time per kg of raw goat not obtained. Chimpanzee g/min at a kill: none found (Gombe, Ngogo, Taï); one Taï case: five chimpanzees ate about 7 kg of colobus in one sitting (no time); per successful hunt Taï males ate a mean 0.48 kg, females 0.13 kg.

**Daily meat intake, wild chimpanzees (annual averages; no hunting-season figure found).** Gombe 55 g/day males, 7 females (Boesch & Boesch-Achermann 2000, quoted); overall 22 g attributed to Wrangham 1975 (Riedel et al. 2020, PeerJ 8283); males under 5% of feeding time on meat. Ngogo 41–55 g males, 10 females (source not named; Watts & Mitani 2002: 850 kg a year for the community). Taï 186 g males (one source 180), 25 females; up to 9% of activity budget. Individual and seasonal variation large; Gombe pregnant and high-ranking females eat more.

**Hominin meat, about 2.5–2.0 Ma (weak).** Bouri (de Heinzelin et al. 1999, *Science*): cut marks about 2.5 Ma, best early butchery evidence (seen only in citing sources). Dikika 3.4 Ma (McPherron et al. 2010, *Nature* 466:857): disputed as trampling (Domínguez-Rodrigo et al. 2012). Kanjera South ~2.0 Ma (Ferraro et al. 2013, *PLoS ONE*): many small antelope carcasses processed. A 2022 *Front Ecol Evol* review (10.3389/fevo.2022.834638): diets varied; meat not the main protein source. **No quantitative meat share or g/day for hominins: any figure is an assumption.** The record shows meat was eaten and butchered, not how much.

**Could not verify:** the 400 kcal/h figure and any range against the paper; Zink & Lieberman chews per kg and which percentages; any chimpanzee g/min; hunting-season intake; the Ngogo source; Stanford and Gilby numbers; Bouri pages; hominin meat quantity.
