# Hominin evidence for growing bodies, strike physics and shells (Fable, 13 Nov 2026; backlog A27)

Reviewer read `CLAUDE.md` first (realism before convenience). Read-only. Main points (full numbers table in the session record;
items marked "from memory" by the reviewer must be checked before use):

- **Adult body mass (hominin):** *A. afarensis* mean 41.0 kg (24.5–63.6), *A. africanus* 30.7, *H. habilis* 33.7, other early
  *Homo* 40.6, *P. boisei* 46.4 (one sure specimen) (Jungers et al. 2016, revising Grabowski et al. 2015). Males about 1.2–1.55 × females
  (McHenry 1992; older method). No mass for *A. garhi* or *P. aethiopicus*.
- **Growth:** no hominin curve; teeth say ape-like, fast timing (first molar in use by 3.4–4.4 years: Smith et al. 2015; Dean et al.
  2001). Mass-by-age shape = chimpanzee stand-in (Gombe), scaled to hominin adults by sex.
- **Hammers:** Lomekwi 3 (3.3 Ma) hammerstones 1.63 and 3.09 kg; Oldowan 2.6 Ma cores about 194 g; experiments 0.3–0.6 kg. Range 0.2–3 kg.
- **Strike energy:** no hominin measure possible; capuchin and chimpanzee/human stand-ins (Liu et al. 2016; Bril et al. 2012).
- **Shells (East Africa):** marula stone 4.66 kN to crush, 2.77 kN to pop the lids; 2.74 kN and **3.28 J** to break in one lab
  (Peters 1993; CIGR Journal 2023). 37 East and southern African species range from under 1 kN to over 20 kN; primates can **bite**
  open anything under about 5.9 kN (Peters 1993).
- **Hands:** nut cracking needs the lowest hand pressure of all stone-tool tasks (Williams-Hatala et al. 2018): grip is not the limit.
- **Did hominins crack nuts at 2.5 Ma? No direct evidence.** Nuts with pitted hammers and anvils: Gesher Benot Ya'aqov, Israel,
  about 780,000 years (Goren-Inbar et al. 2002, *PNAS*; checked by Claude). Older pitted stones at Olduvai have wear that fits nuts,
  meat or bone. *P. boisei* ate mostly C4 grasses or sedges (Cerling et al. 2011).
- **Plant pounding did exist by then:** Nyayanga, Kenya, 3.0–2.6 Ma: Oldowan tools used to cut, scrape and **pound plants** and
  animal tissue, with *Paranthropus* teeth (Plummer et al. 2023, *Science*; checked by Claude).
- **Corrections to the earlier answer:** Coula, Panda and oil palm are West African forest nuts, not East African; marula is the
  measured East African case; teeth are a real route for weaker shells ("stone or nothing" is chimpanzee-biased); build in energy
  (joules), not squeeze force.
- **Would not build yet:** a damage-fatigue law with invented exponents (test both ends instead), hand-anatomy strike rules, separate
  *garhi*/*aethiopicus* bodies, mongongo or doum shells. **Would build:** biting for weaker shells, once Peters's table is read.
