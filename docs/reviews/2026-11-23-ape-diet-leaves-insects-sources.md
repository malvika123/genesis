# What apes and early hominins ate: leaves, pith, insects (23 Nov 2026) — research agent, read only; about 14 searches, no full PDFs: abstracts and secondary summaries

**One line:** leaves and insects were almost certainly a real part of the diet of an ape-like hominin at 2.5 Ma, but the evidence for exact amounts is thin, and the sim's 96% of protein from fruit is far above anything seen in living apes.

| Food | Living savanna chimpanzees (% of feeding time unless said) | Evidence for 2.5 Ma hominins | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ripe fruit | Fongoli about 80% (a 2025 paper, second-hand); Kibale forest 65–75% a year, 35–95% by month (second-hand, citing Wrangham et al.) | teeth and isotopes fit fruit plus other plants; no direct measure | medium |
| Leaves | Fongoli tree leaves 8% or less (second-hand); Kibale pith and young leaves together about 25% (20–45%) in lean months | indirect: inferred from apes; some Early Miocene microwear suggests leaf eating in a few species | medium for "some", low for amount |
| Pith, stems | the main fallback when fruit is scarce (Wrangham, Conklin-Brittain & Hunt 1998); Fongoli eats ground-level plants and pith (no figure) | inference only | medium |
| Flowers, seeds | Fongoli seeds reingested about 2%; flowers listed | hard seeds hinted for some australopiths (afarensis microwear resembles boisei: contested) | low |
| Roots, tubers, sedge corms | Issa and Ugalla woodland plants rise in the late dry season; Ugalla chimpanzees dig roots (Hernandez-Aguilar et al. 2007, from memory) | sedge corms a candidate for the C4 signal (Sponheimer et al. 2013); no direct proof | low-medium |
| Insects (termites, ants) | Fongoli: termites all year, more time than any other population (Bogart & Pruetz 2011); Gombe termite fishing gives energy, fat and some protein, only "modest" (O'Malley & Power 2014) | hints, none firm: bone tools with termite-mound wear at Swartkrans (about 1.8–1.0 Ma, southern Africa: Backwell & d'Errico 2001); isotope tests of termites and sedges (Sponheimer et al. 2005: result unconfirmed) | low-medium |
| Honey | Fongoli: bees and honey late in the dry season (Pruetz 2006) | none | low |
| Meat, eggs | Fongoli: small mammals hunted, some with tools; no percentage | not assessed | — |

**By period (direct evidence).** Miocene apes: microwear puts *Proconsul*, *Sivapithecus*, *Dryopithecus* with soft-fruit eaters and *Ouranopithecus* with hard-object eaters; Early Miocene Kenyan apes show no clear differences (Shearer et al. 2015): weak. *Ardipithecus ramidus* (4.4 Ma): almost no C4 food: mostly C3 plants like modern chimpanzees (White et al. 2009; Sponheimer et al. 2013); Suwa et al. 2009: a partly ground-living omnivore and fruit eater. *A. afarensis*: a large, very variable share of C4/CAM food even within a narrow time slice (Wynn et al. 2013); the C4 signal from about 3.5 Ma. About 2.5 Ma: *Paranthropus* in East Africa moves toward C4 (*P. boisei* about 80% C4 by about 1.9 Ma); *A. africanus* mixed; early *Homo* mixed C3/C4; nothing for *A. garhi*. C4 foods are grasses, sedge corms, and animals or insects that eat them: the signal does not say which. **Direct:** isotopes (plant type, not food class), microwear, tooth shape, Swartkrans bone tools. **Inference from living apes:** every leaf, pith and insect share. **Insects and leaves at 2.5 Ma specifically: no direct evidence either way** (the only insect evidence is southern African and younger).

**Recommended sim diet (ape-like hominin, 2.5 Ma, Awash-type valley; % of feeding time; ranges are the agent's judgement from living apes, not published hominin values).** Ripe fruit 50–75% (start about 60%; savanna chimpanzees' 80% an upper bound); young leaves 5–15% (up to 30% in lean months); pith and stems 3–12% (up to 20% in lean months); roots, tubers, sedge corms 3–15%; seeds, nuts, flowers 2–8%; insects 1–5% by time (higher at termite peaks); honey 0–2% seasonal; eggs, meat, marrow 0–3% (not researched here).
**Build first:** (1) leaves as a real food with protein, eaten more in lean months (the strongest case: every living ape site does it; Kibale chimpanzees keep protein steady while energy varies, about 7:1 non-protein energy to available protein, falling back on young leaves and pith: Uwimbabazi et al. 2021); (2) pith and stems as the dry-season fallback; (3) termites and ants as an ordinary patchy seasonal food gathered by hand first (tool use a later option: chimpanzees do it but there is no direct proof for 2.5 Ma hominins); (4) roots and sedge corms. **Unsupported:** a fixed percentage of insects or leaves for hominins; honey; any claim that the C4 signal proves termites. **Refuters:** leaves: isotope or microwear data showing hominins near 2.5 Ma ate no foliage (unlikely: none exist); termites: proof that the C4 signal comes only from sedges or grass seeds, or no bone tools in East Africa; roots and sedges: wear patterns inconsistent with gritty foods.
**Could not verify:** Pruetz 2006's exact Fongoli percentages; Ugalla, Taï, Gombe, Issa and bonobo food-class percentages; termite kcal and protein per hour and hours per year; Sponheimer et al. 2005's termite result and Lesnik's papers; percent C4 for *afarensis*; any *A. garhi* evidence; Ungar's *afarensis* microwear; microwear for *Ekembo* and *Nacholapithecus*; baobab and underground foods at Fongoli or Ugalla; the Pan–Homo ancestor's diet (Wrangham, Pilbeam); Rothman's protein shares from leaves and insects; meat and eggs at 2.5 Ma.
