# The non-carnivore animals of eastern Africa, 2.8-2.2 Ma: first pass (24 Nov 2026)

**WARNING, read first: UNVERIFIED.** The research agent did not search the web or open any paper. Every number is from memory of the named literature and of modern parks. Use the list of WHICH animals as a working guide; do not code any number until it is checked against its source (a second pass with real searches is needed).

**Summary.** At about 2.8-2.2 Ma eastern Africa had elephants (Elephas recki), hippos, rhinos, giraffids, three pig lineages, hipparion horses, a wide mix of antelopes, large baboons (Theropithecus), crocodiles and fish. The community leaned towards grazers (grassland spreading; Ledi-Geraru about 2.6 Ma almost all grazers: DiMaggio et al. 2015). Sources named (unopened): Bobe & Behrensmeyer 2004; Bobe, Behrensmeyer & Chapman 2002; Reed 1997; Plummer et al. 2015; Cerling et al. 2015; Harris & Cerling 2002; Coe, Cumming & Phillipson 1976.

**Draft table (mass kg; valley-wide density per km2, unverified).** Elephant 3,000-5,000 (0.4); hippo 1,000-3,000 (0.5; pods 10-30, 20-40 per km of river); rhino 800-2,300 (0.1); giraffe/Sivatherium 800-2,000 (0.3); buffalo-like 600-1,000 (1.0; herds 20-300); hipparion horse 250-350 (2; Equus only after about 2.3 Ma); alcelaphines 100-200 (3); impala-like 40-60 (6); reduncines 60-200 (2, at water); tragelaphines 60-250 (0.5); gazelle-like 25-50 (3); oryx-like 150-250 (0.3); pigs 60-250 (3 total); baboons 15-60 (5-15; troops 30-100+); colobines (gallery forest); hares, hyrax, porcupine; ostrich; crocodiles 200-1,000 (a few to tens per river km); fish; tortoise, monitor, snakes.
**Total large-herbivore biomass.** Guess about 5,000 kg/km2 (range 2,000-15,000), clustered near the river; modern parks run about 1,000-30,000 (Coe et al. 1976). Rough split: elephants 30%, hippos 12%, buffalo 11%, equids 11%, alcelaphines 8%, others smaller.
**Dangerous to hominins (modern analogues, not fossil evidence):** hippo, buffalo, elephant, crocodile, rhino. Baboons compete for food and sleep sites.
