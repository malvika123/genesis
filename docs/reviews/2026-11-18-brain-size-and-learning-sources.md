# Brain size and what primates can learn: comparative evidence for the learning-rule question (18 Nov 2026)

Research agent, read only (abstracts and snippets; full texts of several papers blocked), for Opus's safeguard: any change to the learning rule so that more units learn more must be tied to comparative primate data, never to whether brains then grow.
**Comparative data support a link between absolute brain size and cognition: MIXED** — moderate to strong for broad species-level scores, weak and unquantified for the specific skills the sim tests (chain length, cue holding).

**1. Absolute brain size and cognition.** MacLean et al. 2014 (*PNAS* 111:E2140; 567 animals, 36 species, cylinder and A-not-B tasks): absolute brain volume best predicted performance, more than relative size; dietary breadth predicted self-control in primates, group size
did not; Dunbar criticised the tasks; no r or slope retrieved. Deaner, Isler, Burkart & van Schaik 2007 (*Brain Behav Evol* 70:115): absolute brain size the best predictor of a domain-general cognition score; relative measures weak; too few species to settle. Reader & Laland 2002
(*PNAS* 99:4436; about 1,000 studies, 116 primate species): innovation, social learning, tool use correlate with absolute and relative executive brain volume after phylogeny and research effort. Reader, Hager & Laland 2011 (*Phil Trans R Soc B* 366:1017; 62 species): a general-intelligence score tracks
brain volume and captive learning; high scores evolved independently in capuchins, baboons, macaques and great apes. **Caution:** species-level correlations across a huge size range with confounds; they say nothing about how long a chain one individual learns; none tested within the ape range.

**2. Working memory / delayed response (weakly verified).** No cross-species table of maximum delays and no study linking delay length to brain size. Rhesus monkeys above 85% correct at 20 s delays if they could keep orienting toward the baited place (secondary); gorillas fell off from 0 to 90 s (dissertation);
one old chimpanzee study up to about 30 minutes in a variant. Chimpanzee Ai (Kawai & Matsuzawa 2000, *Nature* 403:39): 4 numerals' order over 95%, 5 at 65% (span and speed, not delay). **Delay holding in primates runs from tens of seconds to minutes, with cues and orienting.**

**3. Sequences.** Ai's ordered numerals: about 4–5 items; 6 chimpanzees learned the numeral order 1–9 (Inoue & Matsuzawa 2009). Goualougo chimpanzees (Musgrave, Lonsdorf, Morgan & Sanz 2021): termite fishing from about 2.9 years, brush-tipped probes about 4.3 years, sequential multi-tool use developing through
adolescence; Gombe mastered by about 5.5 years; years with models present. **No study shows larger-brained primates learn longer sequences: unsupported either way.**

**4. Neurons.** Herculano-Houzel 2012 (*PNAS* 109 Suppl 1:10661): the human brain about 86 billion neurons, a scaled-up primate brain by the same rules; total neuron count, not relative size, matters. Cortical counts (recalled, unverified: about 1.7 billion macaque, about 6–7 billion chimpanzee, about 16 billion human): check Herculano-Houzel & Kaas 2011 and the
2012 tables before use. Roth & Dicke not searched.

**5. For the sim.** *Supported:* a mild monotonic link between total neuron count and general learning or innovation across the primate range (a trend across species, not a rule for one chain length). Chimpanzees (about 400 cm³, about the same as early hominins at about 450) manage 4–5 ordered items in lab tasks and need years to learn multi-step tool use in the wild: **a sim chain
cap of 2–3 acts learned without a model sits at or below the chimpanzee level; raising it to roughly 4–5 for chimpanzee-sized brains would match the evidence; above about 5 has no ape support.** *Not supported:* any formula "chain length = k × units"; memory length scaling with units; any gain above chimpanzee level for early hominins; the 64 against 19 unit contrast is outside the range the data
cover; the data cannot separate 27% from 36% of the ceiling. Hold any mapping as a loose range (small, positive, very uncertain; species means, not individuals); tie parameters to chimpanzee ceilings, never pick them to make brains grow. *Refuted by:* a primate study showing species with 2–3× the brain volume learning sequences no longer than chimpanzees; within the sim, a mapping that gives early-hominin brains
more than chimpanzee-level chains or delays.

**Could not verify:** effect sizes (r, slopes) for item 1; cortical neuron counts; any delayed-response comparison across species; step counts in Goualougo tool sets; Roth & Dicke.
