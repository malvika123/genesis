Entry 101: honest watching — a youngster learns only from what it can see of an elder. Written 17 Nov 2026, before any run (design: docs/design/honest-watching.md, Fable's review
docs/reviews/2026-11-17-fable-honest-watching.md; numbers registered as hypotheses in docs/ASSUMPTIONS.md, "Honest watching"). The owner chose (17 Nov): no copying of heard words, no whole-path
copying, same-square watching now, and late or absent skills accepted as an honest answer (never raise the copying rate to rescue them).

Arms (tools/analysis/watch-trace.js: transmission, mother-offspring match, copy events, repeats, deaths; 100 days; 4 processes, nice):
  A old copying (the default)                24 worlds
  B --honest-watching (all six pieces)       24 worlds
  C --no-copying (the floor)                 24 worlds
  D old copying thinned to B's number of copy events (--copy-thin P, P set from B's copy events before D runs)   24 worlds
  single-switch arms, 8 worlds each: --honest-who, --honest-what, --no-path-copy, --no-voice-copy, --honest-hurt, --honest-success
  ends of the hypotheses, 8 worlds each, with --honest-watching: --peer-hi 0.15 --peer-lo 0.05 and --peer-hi 0.25 --peer-lo 0.10; --watch-steps 1 and 4.
Identical worlds: with every switch off, seeds 1-2 x 20 days and seed 1 x 100 days must match the commit before; the new code draws no random numbers then.

What I expect, and why:
1. Copy events (the dose) fall a lot in B: from every step an elder is in sight (A) to a chance of 0.075-0.2 a step when together two steps, awake, by day and foraging: B's copy events per 1,000
   hominin-days are under a fifth of A's.
2. Transmission (first-time rate after watching a skill / before or never watching it): in A the ratio is above 1 for nut, flake and dig but partly because watching and doing happen in the same
   squares (the same place). In B the ratio stays above 1 for at least one skill and is above C's for that skill (C has no watching, so its "watched" count is zero; compare B with D instead:
   B's ratio at least as high as D's, because B copies visible acts with their visible result rather than a choice number).
3. Repeats per 1,000 hominin-days of nut, flake, dig, butcher: B lower than A, closer to C; the share of hominins who ever do each skill lower in B than A.
4. Mother-offspring match: young whose mother did a skill first do it more often than young whose mother never did, in A and B; the gap smaller in B.
5. Deaths, hunger and the population: within noise across A, B, C (copying is a small part of what keeps hominins alive since the honest eye).
6. Single switches: --honest-who and --honest-what carry most of the fall in copy events and repeats; --no-voice-copy, --no-path-copy and --honest-hurt change little in skills.
7. Ends of the hypotheses: more watching (peer 0.25/0.10, watch-steps 1) gives more copy events and more transmission than less (0.15/0.05, watch-steps 4); the order holds.
8. The mother as target: in B, of the young's copy events in their first year (age under 1), the mother is the elder in over 80%. (Read from the copy records if the tool can; otherwise reported as not measured.)

Not held if: B's transmission ratio for every skill is no greater than D's (then what is seen adds nothing beyond fewer events); or B's ratio is no greater than 1 for every skill while A's is above 1
(honest watching passes nothing on: a result, written up as such, not rescued); or deaths or the population move beyond noise between A and B.
Strength warning: first-time events per skill are tens per arm; ratios with fewer than 10 events in either part are traces.

ADDENDUM, 17 Nov, after arms A, B (first build) and C ran and BEFORE the corrected arm runs: the first build applied the sourced watching chance (0.075-0.2 per 15 minutes of ALL time together) only on steps when the
elder was foraging in view, counting the foraging restriction twice (about 0.2 bouts an hour, below the registered 0.3-1.5). That arm is kept as B0 (a low-rate end). Corrected: SOC.peerHi 0.5, SOC.peerLo 0.19 per foraging
step (x 0.92 / 0.37, wild chimpanzee feeding share 36-37%, Waibira). The predictions above apply to the corrected arm B1, unchanged. Already seen in A and C (not a prediction): skills are about as common with no copying
as with old copying; this makes prediction 2's comparison with D (dose-matched) and C the decisive test.

ADDENDUM 2, 17 Nov, before the corrected arm B1 runs: at the owner's question ("grown-ups learn skills too"), an adult can now watch any other adult, older or younger (a youngster still only those older); weakly supported
(Hobaiter 2014; Luncz 2015 summary). Expectation added: in B1 some first-time skill events of adults follow watching a younger adult; adult copy events are a minority of all copy events (adults peer far less).
