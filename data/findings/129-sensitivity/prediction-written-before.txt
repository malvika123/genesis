Entry 129 - sensitivity of the cast test (24 Nov 2026, written before the run). Base = G of entry 127: the E-all switches of entry 126 plus --more-hunters --giant-hyena --african-megantereon --more-herds, 2 of each extra kind (--extra-hunter-n 2), 25 people at the start. Comparison: the seeds 1-12 subset of G-cast (same seeds, same code, written in data/findings/127-cast). 12 real-Earth worlds x 100 days each, seeds 1-12.
Arms: H1 = --extra-hunter-n 1; H4 = --extra-hunter-n 4; B35 = --people 35; B50 = --people 50. All other switches as G.
Purpose: these are SENSITIVITY checks of two numbers that are our own assumptions (a pair of each extra hunter; a band of 25). They are not fixes: the choice of either number must follow evidence (real densities and community sizes; docs/reviews/2026-11-24-real-densities-live.md, 2026-11-24-group-size-and-predation-live.md), not survival.
Predictions: (1) H1 / H4: predator deaths per 1,000 hominin-years scale with the number of extra hunters: H1 about 35 (between the E value 24 and G 46), H4 about 60-75; net change about -2.0% (H1) and -4% to -5.5% (H4); worlds emptied H1 3-6 of 12, H4 6-10 of 12. (2) B35 / B50: predator deaths per 1,000 hominin-years roughly the same as G (within 6): group size does not reliably lower predation within a species (Isbell 1994, 2018; Boesch 1991); hunger deaths rise with band size (competition for the same fruit and meat) by 1-3 points; net change within 1 point of G's; worlds emptied fewer than in G because a bigger band is less likely to be wiped out by chance (G: about 3.5 of 12). (3) Births per 1,000 hominin-years within 3 of G in every arm.
Not held (and then look): predator deaths in B35 or B50 falling by more than 8 (the sim then gives group size a protection that the real record does not show for one species: find the rule that does it); H4 predator deaths rising less than 15 above G (the hunters are saturating on the same prey: find why); any error.
