Entry 113 - births and deaths diagnosed (written 19 Nov 2026 BEFORE the run; step 1 of the population plan, docs/BACKLOG.md "FOCUS"). Measuring only: tools/analysis/life-trace.js on 24 real-Earth worlds (the tool's own default length), pooled by tools/analysis/births-summary.js.
Question: is the decline (-2.7% a year, median world) a births shortfall, a deaths excess, or an age-structure effect? Earlier inference (from deaths about 6% a year and a decline of 2.7%): births about 3.3% a year against 4% or more in real chimpanzees: NOT measured.
Expectations: (1) births per adult female per year about 0.17-0.19 (gaps of 5.3 years, as reality-check.js), which is inside the real range (gaps 5.0-6.5 years): so females who reproduce do so at about the real pace. (2) The share of adult-female time spent 'ready' (not pregnant or nursing) is small, and a ready female is usually near a ready male (above 70%): so mating opportunity is not the limit. (3) Therefore the births shortfall, if any, comes from FEW females reproducing: early ages (age at first birth 15.5 years against 13-14.6 in real chimpanzees, because the sim's first mating age is 14 where the registered source is for first BIRTH), and adult females dying before they reproduce or between births, with infants then lost (a mother's death kills the dependent infant: 28 of 56 baby deaths had the mother already dead, entry 104).
Not held if: births per adult female per year are well below 0.15 (then the gaps are not the real pace: something delays conception); or ready females are often without a ready male near (then it is mating opportunity).
Nothing is changed; no number is tuned. If (3) holds, step 2 is a correction of the first-mating age to the source (first mating = first birth minus pregnancy), a sourced correction written with its own prediction and tested at both ends.
